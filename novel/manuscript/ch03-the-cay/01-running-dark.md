It was a long while before anyone spoke. The outpost shrank astern to a scatter of lamps and then to nothing.

“Domingo,” I said. Not a question.

“In the trees above the town. He’d not meet me anywhere with walls.” Crumble had his eyes on the water ahead. “Garrison came straight up the path. Somebody sold the meeting before it happened.”

“And you got out.”

“Put a smoke down and went into the green. Domingo stood up out of it so they’d have a man to take.” His hands stayed easy on the tiller. “Last I saw, they had him on his knees and the Spaniard in the clean coat was looking at the trees. Right at them.” He let a breath out. “So I came down to the boat.”

I did not tell him he was right to leave. He knew he was right to. There is nothing crueler you can tell a man than that he was right to leave his friend on his knees in the dark, and nothing truer, so I said neither, and let the wind have it.

“You get what you went for?” Bonesy said. He’d come aft from the cable, the new hat a black shape against the stars.

“Part of it. It’s real, and it’s broken, and the pieces run in a line, like the marks on a chart. The cave off Jigsaw’s island is the first mark. Find it right and it sets you the bearing to the next.”

“And he gave you that bearing?”

“He was getting to it in the trees when the garrison came up the path.”

So we had the first mark and no leg drawn past it, and the one man who could have laid the rest of the course was on his knees in Tortuga. Bonesy looked back the way we’d come, north and east, where the lamps had been.

“They’ll not sit on that long,” he said. “A man like the one you’ve drawn, he’ll have a hull behind us by the morning watch.”

“Let them,” Crumble said. “Nobody on this sea catches her with a clean bottom and a fair wind.”

“They will if it blows,” I said. “We run from weather. The kind of ship he’ll send—”

“Sails straight through it,” Bonesy said. He said it lightly, the way he says the things that aren’t, and took the weight of it off me, which is a thing he does more often than I let on.

Nobody answered that. There was nothing to answer. It went into the boat with the rest of the night’s weight and we left it there.

“We’ll be thirsty before any of it,” I said, to put us back on a thing we could mend. “We left the water on the quay. A day aboard, maybe less.”

“There’s the cacao,” Crumble said.

The pressed tablets the Mayans carried, that he’d had the sense to lay in. It would keep us standing if it kept us nothing else, and tasted, he allowed, like the scrapings off a wet deck. But cacao is dry, and a man cannot drink it dry.

“We water before the island. Off the run, nowhere they’ll watch.” I ran the cays in my head, which held and which went dry. “There’s a string of low ones west and south. Most are sand and nothing. But there’s one the reef wraps the whole windward face of, and that one I never made, the gap looked mean and I had water enough at the time not to chance it.”

“The one with the palms standing in a hook,” Bonesy said.

I looked at him.

“I’ve been to it.”

He has been to nearly every scrap of land in this sea and remembers almost none of them. The ones he keeps are not the ones where men took him in, because men never did. A walking dead thing is the first over the side when a boat runs short, and he has been put over the side of more boats than I’ve sailed. What he keeps is the stone. A face in a wall that did not decide he was expendable, that did not leave.

“There’s a spring behind the rocks at the back,” he said. “And a face the old people cut over it. Ixchel. I sat with her a season once.” A beat. “Worse company than Beatriz, and Beatriz is a post.”

“Then the water’s clean,” Crumble said. “The Mayans cut a face over a spring to mark it safe.”

So between us we had it. My reckoning of the reef and his of the stone. One gap in that windward face, the surge standing in it like a wall, a boat through at first light or not at all. She’d go through. I’d put her through. I kept the rest in my head.

I took the first watch. Crumble would not sleep and Bonesy never does, and so we went on three men awake on a small boat, the way we’d come in, lighter by all our water and heavier by everything else, and one eye, all of us now, on the dark astern.
