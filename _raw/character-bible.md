# Character Bible: The Codex Trilogy, Book One

Caribbean, 1690. First person, narrated by Jigsaw, plain sailor's voice. Tonal register after *Gideon the Ninth*: sardonic, raunchy, genuinely dark, pathos buried under comedy.

This file is the writing reference for the three leads, the antagonist, and the offscreen dead. It holds what was established and everything settled in development. The aim is to write each character from the inside every time, without guessing.

---

## How to use this file

Read the thematic core and the scene machine first. They govern everything. Then each character section gives role, backstory, wound, the lie, voice, tells, and a short "how to write him" to keep open while drafting. The timeline at the end reconciles all the dates.

Two rules sit above all the rest:

1. Emotional beats are shown through behavior, never stated. A man who says it is fine and means it does not say it.
2. Every analogy comes from the pirates' world. Ocean, fish, weather, hide, stone, nature. No modern phrasing. 1690 is locked.

---

## The thematic core

The question the trilogy asks: can two broken men find something worth living for in each other, and is that enough?

Jigsaw seeks meaning through the spiritual unknown, the door at the bottom of the sea. Crumble seeks it through Balam's unfinished work. Both chase the same thing without knowing it, and the thing is the bond between the three of them and Bonesy. That meaning is designed to literally unlock the final codex gate at the trilogy's end. The emotional arc and the mystical mechanic are one and the same.

Bonesy already found it, long ago. He is the immortal who learned what the brothers are still chasing, that meaning lives in company that means something. He watches them look for theirs.

The wounds are not flavor. They are the lock.

---

## The mirror (Jigsaw and Crumble)

The two brothers are the same wound from opposite ends.

Jigsaw is the abandoned one. A woman he believed he would marry left him for a higher man. He was not enough to keep her.

Crumble is the abandoner. He left a woman who loved him, to go prove he was meant for more, and she did fine without him. He mattered so little that the leaving cost the world nothing.

The shared spine under both: *I did not matter the way I needed to.* That sits under their silences. They know each other's headline, not the details, and the not asking is the respect. They do not refuse to look. When one wound is bared and it rhymes with the other's, the second man is present to it, even with no word said. Witnessed, not discussed. It lives in small things, eyes kept on the luff, a tiller moved a hair it did not need.

They share one beat exactly, and that is the engine of the mirror. Each man went back to the woman and found her settled with another man, and in each case the other man is the very thing that beat him. Crumble found a plain nobody who stayed himself and got everything, which is unbearable because Crumble left to become more than a nobody. Jigsaw found a bought officer with blood and coin, the thing a sailor can never become. Same return. Opposite verdicts. One says *you were never anything special and you didn't need to leave.* The other says *you were never going to be enough no matter what you did.*

About seven years of brotherhood now, from meeting as very young men at Tortuga, with the years they spent apart folded in the middle.

## The scene machine (division of labor)

Most scenes with the three run on the same engine.

**Bonesy drives the noise.** He pries. He says what the two repressed men will not, does what people think and sit on. No shame, no stake, which is the tool that opened their wounds in the first place. He is the reason they know each other's headlines. He talked it out of them across the year.

**Crumble bleeds when pried.** He shuts the door, says it is fine, reaches for a task, the tiller, a line, the chart. Bonesy finds the gap anyway. The task does not save him.

**Jigsaw threads.** He is the seam the reader gets context through. He speaks a little, then the narration turns the moment outward to the world or inward to himself. His asides give the reader more of him, the world, and the other two. When the moment touches his own wound, it leaks, and it should click as personal in the moment, not as a reread surprise.

Dialogue ladder by volume: Bonesy well above the others, then Crumble, then Jigsaw a notch under Crumble. Jigsaw is quietest in the room, loudest on the page, because we live in his head.

## JIGSAW — narrator, the one who sees

### Role and domain
The perceptive one. His domain is the physical world: water, weather, currents, the small wrong thing in a room. He keeps the trio alive by reading what others miss. Narration is plain, observational, withholding, a sailor's mouth.

### Heritage and backstory
One quarter Middle Eastern, through his father. The Barbary corsairs never crossed to the Caribbean, so the blood rides in on one man who did. His father was of that North African corsair world, a man who came over, bought a plantation, and buried his past to pass the Spanish blood laws, limpieza de sangre, which demanded proof of no Muslim, Jewish, or recent native ancestry. The father had connections enough to pass the test and nothing past that. He trained his son in the water and in sailing before the son ever knew why the sea was in his blood. He gave him a ring the day before he first shipped out, and never told him what it meant.

Jigsaw went to a good Spanish school and always felt a touch off for his heritage, though he did not much look it. The school fed its best into Spanish service at sea, and he was one of the best, a sharp mind and an elite blade from underground duels in his youth. He believed he would marry a beautiful Spanish girl. Everything was laid out.

At 16 he went to sea in Spanish service. He found it wrong from the start: promotions bought, the rigidity, the crew with no rights. At 16 and a half a patrol crossed a merchant ship, and a Muslim merchant aboard marked the ring and muttered the very words his father always muttered. Jigsaw understood his father had kept something from him. Off duty in Maracaibo, at a back room house of the kind sailors pay into, the owner saw the ring and put his guards on him, then eased the moment Jigsaw showed it and said his father's saying. The man took him to dinner and opened the secret to him. The ring is the mark of a hidden brotherhood (NAME TO BE DECIDED), men of the corsair and Morisco world who came to the New World under false Christian names, carrying their sea skills, leaving their past behind. He spoke of leaders who vanished, claiming to have found enlightenment, said to have been consumed or dispersed in some mystical way. That is the first seed of Jigsaw's spiritual hunger.

At 17 he went home to find both parents sick with malaria. His father, barely able to speak, gave him the name of a local blacksmith, another of the brotherhood, who knew of Jigsaw only because the father had once told a fellow Muslim of his son. The blacksmith gave him stories of his father, of how the man left his past when his son was born, mended his Spanish swords, and told him of a place where a crew runs fair, where a man can find both riches and greater meaning, half myth. His parents died of the malaria because they could not afford the cure, having spent their means on his schooling, while the wealthy bought the medicine and lived. His classmates with rich fathers became officers. The girl he loved married one of them, a man who bought his commission. Jigsaw left wanting to learn one thing: how to make the Spanish suffer. Then Tortuga, where he met Crumble.

### The wound
The girl, the parents, the blood laws, the bought ranks, all of it. The cruelty grows from a real grievance. But write it as a flaw, not justice. He often takes it out on Spanish men who did nothing to him, and that is the rot in him, the wound paying itself forward. The narration knows this. It is selective, aimed at the soft handed, high born, never worked kind of Spaniard, the kind who beat him on blood and coin. The tell is already on the page: in the tavern he clocks the spy entirely by clean, soft, never worked hands. That contempt is the wound talking.

### The deeper hunger
Beneath adventure and gold, he is searching for meaning, the door at the bottom of the sea he stood at when he was twenty and did not go through. He frames it as adventure. The corsair blood and the tale of vanished enlightened men resonate under it, but keep the door itself unnamed and his own. He wants the codex most, which is why his counsel of caution carries weight. The mystical blood is a seeded thread, accounted for later, not explained now.

### The lie he tells himself
*I go for the gold and the door.* The truth: the water is the one place the status that beat him counts for nothing. Down there a soft handed don is worth less than a man who can read a current. His search for meaning is partly a search for a world where the thing that beat him does not count.

### Voice and dialogue
Not mute. He banters with Bonesy, pushes back on Crumble, keeps a dry mouth. He lands slightly fewer lines than Crumble, a notch, not a gulf. Because his lines are rarer they land harder and should cost him. We should know him best of the three.

### Appearance and gear
Early twenties, a year older than Crumble. Razor shaved head, a scar on his face, hide built pirate rig where salt eats wool and where a blade looks to land, oiled dark, not worn like a hunter. He carries twin lighter Spanish arming swords, his own from his dueling and service youth, mended by the brotherhood blacksmith, so they are half him. (NOTE: this revises the written line that he took them off dead men. The new origin fits the book's idea that a man's gear is half him. Veto if you want the old line.) A legend follows him from a galleon he cleared single handed.

### How to write him
He says little aloud and tells us much. In two handers he is the third instrument, threading observation through the gaps, the wound leaking personal in the moment. His skill came the slow way, practice and watching and trying, then a sword that argued for him after Crumble left, then the solo years exploring this sea alone in a small boat, because some things no man teaches you. His eye stays always on the physical world. He notices everything and says only some of it.

## CRUMBLE — the Maya trained one, the keeper of history

### Role and domain
Early twenties, a year younger than Jigsaw. His domain is the cultural and historical: the Maya language, the old stone, the codex, what things mean. Trained by Balam through his late teens into his early twenties, after he and Jigsaw were already at sea. He carried the codex knowledge passively until Balam's death about a year and four months back gave it nowhere to go.

### Backstory and the wound
He left a woman who loved him, at 16, for the sea. Not simply for adventure. He left to prove he was meant for more, to make a prize of his own, because he had done nothing and carried the quiet certainty that he was nothing, an imposter's hunger. Deep down he always hoped she would wait.

He came to Tortuga, joined the crew, and met Jigsaw. Three years at sea together. He learned real skill from his first captain and did decently, enough that he could have bought a small farm and been a settled man. On a raid he saved a Maya woman, a civilian, out of plain decency, and she turned out to be Balam's daughter. She told him of her father, that Crumble was meant for more, and that he should seek the man out if he had it in him.

At about 19 he went back to the woman. He found her happy, married young, a child, settled with an ordinary man, a nobody just like Crumble had been before, special in nothing except that he stayed and was himself. That broke something. Not only her being happy. The man. After a few days watching her and her family from the edge of it, he went to seek more than adventure, and he went to Balam.

So she is alive and well now, the worst version, fine without him, and the leaving holds no tragedy, only waste. He threw something good away to chase being more, and a man who chased nothing got everything by standing still.

### Two engines (write both)
Penance and proof. The penance: he decided he would never again be the man who throws people away, which is why he is loyal past sense, and loyalty to Balam is the ledger he can still pay into. The proof: the imposter's hunger never died, and Balam is the man who looked at him and said he was meant for more. Finishing Balam's work proves both that he is more than a nobody and that he is not a man who discards what matters. The two run together.

### The arc inside the wound
He believes the leaving was a mistake. Across the story he comes to hold it instead as a choice he made, and to look at what it bought him, the sea, the brother, Bonesy, Balam's errand. Bonesy models that destination for him without either naming it, poor trade, still, something. The hanging on to old promises is the penance still running, and the end of his arc is learning to set a promise down on purpose, the way he should have chosen the woman on purpose.

### The lie he tells himself
*Loyalty is just who I am.* The truth: it is penance, and proof.

### The cover move
Touched, he goes silent, says it is fine, reaches for the next task. Bonesy gets through anyway. Write the reach for the task, then the task failing to save him. The "it is fine" is the tell, and he answers against his will when pried close, which says the wound sits nearer the surface than he would like.

### Defiance
He wears the Maya openly, worked cloth and plate over the European coat, a deep red sash, a blade Balam had made for him, older and shorter than a cutlass. He walks into Spanish rooms looking like the thing forty years of Cárdenas has been spent trying to scrub off the earth, knowing exactly what he looks like. The bravest small thing Jigsaw knows a man to do daily.

### Voice
Terse, controlled, a man sitting at his own wake. He speaks Maya with a softening only the trusted hear. He chews a tobacco leaf the way Balam taught. He and Jigsaw build off each other, neither simply shutting the other down. Grief held in a controlled body, the feeling in the hands and the jaw, never in a speech.

## OLD BONESY — the immortal, the comic engine, the secret heart

### Role
The id, and the emotional id both. A strong read on people, real wisdom, and extremely carefree and humble with it. He says what the two will not and does what humans think and sit on. He pried both their wounds open across the year and is the reason they each know the other's headline. He talked it out of them by never shutting up and having no shame and no stake.

### The hormone monster function (1690 register, not the modern one)
Appetite, no shame, no equipment. He names the lust and the greed out loud, eggs the two repressed men toward their wants, leers with nothing to leer with. The running joke is a skeleton with all the appetite and none of the parts. The register is on the page already in the mermaid line, the one end of her that turns a coin. Bawdy, bodily, of its century. Never modern.

### Limits
He will not be cruel for no reason. He crosses every line, but every crossing has a point, getting the two to the thing they need to say or see. The carelessness is the delivery, never the intent. No sacred topic, but a purpose under every touch.

### The arc
Early he pokes the wounds mostly for sport, entertained by the two sad men, caring a little. Watching them grieve the lost finger because it was educational is exactly this man. As the story unfolds he sympathizes far more. He still pokes, same flip voice, but you see him choose the moment and pull up short when it has gone far enough. The care never gets said. It shows in the timing.

### The wound underneath
Centuries treated as expendable, the first thing over the side when a boat ran short. Put over the side of more boats than Jigsaw has sailed. He gravitated to stone because stone does not leave. His friendships with carvings (Beatriz, the stone Ixchel, Margaret the saint's finger) are a lonely immortal making companions of things that cannot abandon him. With these two he has, for the first time, the real thing, people who would buy him a hat.

### The lie he tells himself
*I prefer stone. Stone does not leave.* Already cracked by the hat, which is right, since he is furthest along. The cord he half reaches for and does not touch is him loosening his grip on the substitutes.

### Voice (get the rhythm exact)
High pitched, raspy, drawn out. Common words only, never elevated, because he was a peasant, not a wit. He does not build speeches. Short lines. Pauses. He pries with annoying questions, not theses. Comma loose, a bit shorter, less structured. Smart and wise but common.

Rhythm test:

Wrong, too built: "I'm only watching the two of you not want things. It's hard labor to watch. You want a thing and you sit on it like a hen on a egg and nothing ever hatches and you call that being a man."

Right, his rhythm: short line, pause, then he just asks. "You had a woman." ... "What was her name." ... "She set you down, or you set her down."

### His role in the theme
He already learned meaning lives in the bond. He found his long ago, and he is quietly building the same thing with these two, which is what the hat is about.

## DON MATEO DE CÁRDENAS — the antagonist

Old, fifties and up, older than the two and still a dangerous fighter. Forty years a Spanish colonial officer, Peru young, then the Maya regions. Not a personal vendetta. A colonialist ideologue who believes the world should be Spanish and Christian and takes out whatever is neither, with calculated cruelty, torture, breaking the people around his targets.

### The Thrawn dimension
He collects the histories, books, and sacred artifacts of the peoples he destroys, studies them, understands them better than they understand themselves, because you cannot truly finish a people until you do. This makes him able to assemble the codex's shape from fragments. He keeps occult artifacts of his own, though curses are anomalous even here.

### The threat
He does not appear in chases. He plans, watches, runs spies, and was already ahead of the trio at Tortuga. He commands ships that sail straight through the storms the trio must flee, which nullifies their one advantage. The not being dead after forty years is the frightening part.

### The personal thread
The bought officer who took Jigsaw's woman rose into Cárdenas's orbit. This binds the codex enemy and the personal enemy into one, a reveal that can land hard later. Jigsaw has some idea of it already, which is why reading up on Cárdenas was never only tactical.

## The dead and the offscreen

**Balam.** Crumble's teacher, a Maya resistance fighter and master who taught him fighting, stealth, history, and language. Died naturally about a year and four months ago, mid hunt for the codex. Lives in memory. One ghost appearance possible later if it earns its place.

**Balam's daughter.** The Maya woman Crumble saved on a raid, who sent him to her father. She continues in the story as another living piece of Balam.

**Domingo.** Maya contact at Tortuga, knew Balam as a boy, goes by a Spanish name in port. Taken by Cárdenas's soldiers so Crumble could escape. Fate is an open thread.

**Jigsaw's woman.** The Spanish girl he believed he would marry, lost to the bought officer now in Cárdenas's orbit.

**Crumble's woman.** Alive, married, a young child, fine without him. He left her at 16 to prove he was more. Not a ghost, not a return. The wound is her doing well, and the ordinary man who got her.

## Timeline (reconciles all dates)

**Jigsaw**
- 16: to sea in Spanish service.
- 16 and a half: the patrol, the merchant ship, the ring marked by a Muslim merchant. Later, Maracaibo, the house owner who opens the brotherhood's secret to him.
- 17: home to dying parents, the blacksmith and the mended swords, parents dead of malaria, the girl married to the bought officer. Then Tortuga.

**Crumble**
- 16: leaves the woman, comes to Tortuga, joins the crew, meets Jigsaw.
- 16 to 19: three years at sea in the same crew. Saves Balam's daughter on a raid in this stretch.
- about 19: returns to the woman, finds her married with a child to an ordinary man. Goes to Balam.
- about 19 to 22: trains under Balam.

**Together**
- They meet at Tortuga, Jigsaw 17, Crumble 16. The crew is the same crew for both.
- Three years in that crew, then about three years apart, Crumble training under Balam, Jigsaw letting his swords speak and then sailing alone to explore this sea.
- Reunite as the mercenaries we meet now. Present age early twenties, in the 23 to 25 range, Jigsaw the elder by a year.
- Balam dies about a year and four months before the book opens.

This corrects the written line that placed their meeting at sixteen on the Mosquito Coast across fifteen years. Birthplace of the brotherhood is now Tortuga, and the span is about seven years.

## Quick reference: voice rules per character

**Jigsaw.** Plain sailor's mouth. Dry. A notch quieter than Crumble, never mute. Asides do the heavy carrying. Analogies from water, fish, weather, hide. Wound leaks personal in observation.

**Crumble.** Terse, controlled, grief in the body not the mouth. Maya softening only the trusted hear. Says it is fine and reaches for a task when cornered. Builds off Jigsaw, never just shuts him down.

**Bonesy.** High, raspy, drawn out. Common words only. Short lines, pauses, pries with questions. Comma loose, less structured. Bawdy, bodily, of its century. Never elevated, never a built speech.

## How wounds leak (the mechanics)

- Show through behavior, never state. Hands, jaw, where the eyes go, what gets reached for.
- A wound is touched when someone gets near it. Crumble shuts the door, Bonesy finds the gap, Jigsaw threads the meaning out to the reader.
- When a bared wound rhymes across the mirror, the other brother is present to it without a word. Witnessed, not discussed.
- Jigsaw's leaks read personal in the moment because we are in his head. The others leak only what they let slip, which is little.
- Wounds emerge organically from scenes. Do not pre plan the exposition.

---

## Register and anachronism rules (locked)

- 1690 is the date. No modern phrasing, ever.
- All analogies from the pirates' world: ocean, fish, nature, weather, hide, stone, rope.
- The father's people are grounded in the Morisco and Barbary corsair world. Do not use "maroon," which means escaped African captives, a different people, and do not use "guerrilla," which is not a word until the 1800s.
- Watch the small things. Past flags caught: boot laces, modern phrases, anachronistic references like "a fair."
- Bonesy never speaks in elevated register. He was a peasant.
- Edits go into the full passage being worked on, never shown in isolation.

---

## Open threads to decide

- Name for the father's secret brotherhood, to replace the wrong terms. Marked to be named.
- Whether the bought officer who took Jigsaw's woman is truly the stronger, sharper man or built into that by the wound. If he matters later, make him real.
- Whether the new sword origin (his own, mended by the blacksmith) replaces the written line about taking them off dead men.
- When and how the full Cárdenas link lands, for Jigsaw and for the reader.
- Whether Balam earns a single ghost appearance, and how his daughter returns.
- Domingo's fate.
- The scene that carries Crumble's reframe from mistake to chosen.

---

## The one line test

Before writing any of the three, ask: is the feeling shown in behavior, is every comparison from their world, and is the voice on the right rung of the ladder. If yes, it holds.
