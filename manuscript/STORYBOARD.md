# Book One: storyboard

`OUTLINE.md` is what is written. This is what is planned. Where a thing is
settled it is stated flat. Where it is not, it says so and says what is waiting
on it. Nothing in here was invented to fill a gap.

---

## What the trilogy is for

From the bible, settled:

The question is whether two broken men can find something worth living for in
each other, and whether that is enough. Jigsaw chases it through the door at
the bottom of the sea. Crumble chases it through Balam's unfinished work. Both
are chasing the bond between the three of them and Bonesy without knowing it,
and that bond is designed to open the final codex gate at the end of book
three. The emotional arc and the mystical mechanic are the same mechanism. The
wounds are the lock.

Bonesy already found it, long ago, and watches the other two look.

## What book one has to do

Settled, by implication rather than by decision. Worth confirming:

- Plant that the locks are not locks, so the ending of book three is earned
  rather than sprung. (`notes/open-threads.md`)
- Put Cárdenas on the board as a man who assembles, not a man who chases.
- Get the three of them from a failed job to the first piece of the codex.
- Move Crumble one step from *the leaving was a mistake* toward *the leaving
  was a choice*. The bible calls this his arc. No scene carries it yet.

## The spine

What is actually established about the plot, in order, with sources.

1. **The codex is real, broken, and sequential.** Crumble, 3.1: *it's real, and
   it's broken, and the pieces run in a line, like the marks on a chart.*
2. **The cave off Jigsaw's island is the first mark.** Crumble, 3.1. *Find it
   right and it sets you the bearing to the next.*
3. **Domingo had the bearing to the second and was taken before he gave it.**
   3.1, and `story-so-far.md`.
4. **Cárdenas holds Domingo.** He keeps the histories of the people he
   destroys, and Domingo is a keeper, which makes him worth more alive.
   (`notes/open-threads.md`, marked as reasoning, not as a decision.)
5. **Cárdenas commands ships that sail through the weather the trio must run
   from.** Their one advantage is void. (bible, The threat)
6. **Cárdenas tortures the bearing out of Domingo and is waiting at the second
   mark.** The three come out of the cave with the bearing and sail it. He has
   had it longer and sails through the weather they run from, so he is there
   first. Aydin, 2026-10-11.

That is the whole of it. Six facts.

## Open, and blocking

Each of these stops the draft somewhere specific.

### 1. Where does book one end?

Nothing anywhere says. Everything below depends on it. Candidates the material
suggests, none of them chosen:

- At the first piece of the codex, with the bearing to the second lost.
- At the second mark, with Cárdenas arriving.
- At Domingo, alive or dead, in Cárdenas's hands.

**Blocks:** the shape of chapters 4 through the end, and whether chapter 4 as
drafted is a middle or a climb.

### 2. What is behind the water?

The outline says a cave and the first mark. Nothing says what is in it, what
reaching it costs, or what it gives them.

**Blocks:** the next scene. This is the immediate one.

### 3. Does Domingo's bearing ever reach them, and how?

**Settled, 2026-10-11.** It reaches Cárdenas, by torture. The three get their
bearing from the cave. Still open: whether Domingo is alive at the second mark,
and who tells Crumble what happened to him.

### 4. Does Cárdenas know where they went?

Raised by the 4.1 draft and unresolved. Every man on the ship at the cay died.
Nobody reported. Either the wreck is found, or a ship was expected and its
silence is the message, or the three of them are simply wrong about what he
knows, which is a different and more interesting book.

**Settled by fact 6.** It is a race. Cárdenas does not need to know where they
went. He knows where they are going.

### 5. The scene that carries Crumble's reframe

The bible names it as his arc and no scene does it. Bonesy is supposed to model
the destination without either of them naming it.

**Blocks:** nothing mechanical. Blocks the book being about what it says it is
about.

### 6. Smaller, already logged

In `notes/open-threads.md`: whether Balam gets a ghost appearance, how his
daughter returns, whether Bonesy names the finger Margaret, the name of the
father's brotherhood, whether the bought officer is made real.

## Under discussion, nothing chosen

Aydin's ideas from 2026-10-11, with what the page already carries for each.

### Domingo as Cárdenas's informant

The page half wrote this already:

- 2.2, Crumble: *He keeps the ears of half the coast and lends them cheap to
  people he likes.* A man who sells what he hears.
- 2.2, Crumble: *a Maya name in a Spanish port is a thing men collect you for.*
  Leverage, if Cárdenas knows the real name.
- 3.1, Crumble: *Somebody sold the meeting before it happened.*
- 3.1, Crumble: *Domingo stood up out of it so they'd have a man to take.*

A shape that uses all four: Domingo sells Cárdenas small things to stay
uncollected. He sells the meeting. He will not sell the bearing, because Balam
was his friend as a boy. In the trees he gives Crumble the cave and holds the
bearing back, because anything said aloud reaches the clean coat. When the
garrison comes he stands up so Crumble can run. Cárdenas then tortures out of
him the one thing he would not sell.

Cost: the spy in 2.3 matters less if Cárdenas already had a man inside.
Possible answer: the spy is how Cárdenas learned the meeting was tonight, and
he leaned on Domingo that afternoon.

### The vision: Balam and Jigsaw's father

Two placements, tied to how book one closes:

- **In the cave.** Bad air in a sealed cave, what miners of the century called
  damp. Jigsaw and Crumble breathe it. Bonesy does not breathe and sees nothing.
  The father appearing there ties the cave to the brotherhood's leaders who
  vanished claiming enlightenment (bible, Jigsaw backstory).
- **In Cárdenas's hold.** He gives them a sacred plant taken from a people he
  destroyed, datura or similar, and sits and listens while they talk to their
  dead. He learns their wounds the way he learns a people. Only works if book
  one ends with them in his hands.

Note: the bible has the father dead of malaria. If "held by Cárdenas" means the
father alive, that is a bible change and needs a decision.

### Cárdenas's collection

Candidates, all real to the century. Bonesy has already been in one of his
cellars at Veracruz, *where the dead ones were kept* (1.2), so any of these can
be paid off by a line from him.

- An obsidian scrying mirror from Mexico. One reached John Dee in England in
  the 1500s.
- A quipu, Inca knotted cord, from the Peru years. He can read it.
- A bezoar stone he drops in his cup against poison.
- Saints' relics and idols on one shelf. The finger came out of his cellar.
- A stone face Bonesy sat with once, now on his shelf. A stone that was taken.
- A second cursed thing, kept in a box. Why he looks at Bonesy the way he does.
