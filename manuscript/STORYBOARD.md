## What the trilogy is for

From the bible, settled:

The question is whether two broken men can find something worth living for in
each other, and whether that is enough. Jigsaw chases it through the door at
the bottom of the sea. Crumble chases it through Balam's unfinished work. Both
are chasing the bond between the three of them and Bonesy without knowing it.

Revised by Aydin, 2026-10-17: the bond is no longer the key to the codex. The
codex follows old, impersonal rules (leaves, a living day count, the rite, the
place, the time). The bond matters because it makes them able to trust each
other with work those rules give to several hands at once.

Bonesy already found it, long ago, and watches the other two look.

## What book one has to do

Settled, by implication rather than by decision. Worth confirming:

- Put Cárdenas on the board as a man who assembles, plans and breaks people,
  with a chain a reader can follow every time he finds them.
- Get the three of them from a failed job to the first piece of the codex.
- Move Crumble one step from *the leaving was a mistake* toward *the leaving
  was a choice*. The bible calls this his arc. No scene carries it yet.

## The spine

What is actually established about the plot, in order, with sources.

1. **The codex is real, broken, and sequential.** Crumble, 3.1: *it's real, and
   it's broken, and the pieces run in a line, like the marks on a chart.*
2. **The cave off Jigsaw's island is the first mark.** Crumble, 3.1. *Find it
   right and it sets you the bearing to the next.*
3. **Domingo had the bearing to the second and was taken before he gave it.**
   3.1, and `story-so-far.md`.
4. **Cárdenas holds Domingo.** He keeps the histories of the people he
   destroys, and Domingo is a keeper, which makes him worth more alive.
   (`notes/open-threads.md`, marked as reasoning, not as a decision.)
5. **Cárdenas commands ships that sail through the weather the trio must run
   from.** Their one advantage is void. (bible, The threat)
6. **Cárdenas tortures the bearing out of Domingo and is waiting at the second
   mark.** The three come out of the cave with the bearing and sail it. He has
   had it longer and sails through the weather they run from, so he is there
   first. Aydin, 2026-10-11.
7. **Domingo was Cárdenas's informant.** He sold the meeting and would not sell
   the bearing. Aydin, 2026-10-11. Shape below.
8. **The three end up in Cárdenas's hold, and the vision happens there.**
   Jigsaw and Crumble see Balam and Jigsaw's father. The father is still dead,
   as the bible has it. What they see is a vision, not a man. Aydin, 2026-10-11.
9. **Cárdenas uses magic against the peoples of the Caribbean, and his senior
   officers carry artifacts.** Aydin, 2026-10-11. Rules not yet set, below.
10. **The book is set in 1670, and the chapter 2 outpost is Petit-Goâve.**
    Tortuga is at its height and is saved for later. Aydin, 2026-10-11.
11. **Cárdenas is just starting on the buccaneers.** His twenty-odd years went into
    other conquest, on land. Aydin, 2026-10-11.

12. **The codex, made whole, unmakes every curse and strange thing.** That is
    why Cárdenas wants it: with the strange gone, the empires take the islands as
    in real history. It would unmake Bonesy. Aydin, 2026-10-16.
13. **Balam's daughter joins and helps Crumble, and the two slowly fall in
    love.** Aydin, 2026-10-16.
14. **Domingo was taken because he lacks Crumble's training.** He is a plot
    device for broken loyalty and for getting Cárdenas to the second mark.
    Aydin, 2026-10-16.
15. **Each lead has a named drive** (bible): Jigsaw rage, Crumble obligation and
    regret, Bonesy crude open-minded humor, Cárdenas control born of anxiety.
    Aydin, 2026-10-16.

That is the whole of it. Fifteen facts.

## Open, and blocking

Each of these stops the draft somewhere specific.

### 1. Where does book one end?

Nothing anywhere says. Everything below depends on it. Candidates the material
suggests, none of them chosen:

- At the first piece of the codex, with the bearing to the second lost.
- At the second mark, with Cárdenas arriving.
- At Domingo, alive or dead, in Cárdenas's hands.

**Blocks:** the shape of chapters 4 through the end, and whether chapter 4 as
drafted is a middle or a climb.

### 2. What is behind the water?

The outline says a cave and the first mark. Nothing says what is in it, what
reaching it costs, or what it gives them.

**Blocks:** the next scene. This is the immediate one.

### 3. Does Domingo's bearing ever reach them, and how?

**Settled, 2026-10-11.** It reaches Cárdenas, by torture. The three get their
bearing from the cave. Still open: whether Domingo is alive at the second mark,
and who tells Crumble what happened to him.

### 4. Does Cárdenas know where they went?

Raised by the 4.1 draft and unresolved. Every man on the ship at the cay died.
Nobody reported. Either the wreck is found, or a ship was expected and its
silence is the message, or the three of them are simply wrong about what he
knows, which is a different and more interesting book.

**Settled by fact 6.** It is a race. Cárdenas does not need to know where they
went. He knows where they are going.

### 5. The scene that carries Crumble's reframe

The bible names it as his arc and no scene does it. Bonesy is supposed to model
the destination without either of them naming it.

**Blocks:** nothing mechanical. Blocks the book being about what it says it is
about.

### 6. Smaller, already logged

In `notes/open-threads.md`: whether Balam gets a ghost appearance, how his
daughter returns, whether Bonesy names the finger Margaret, the name of the
father's brotherhood, whether the bought officer is made real.

## Settled 2026-10-11, details still open

### Domingo, the informant

The page half wrote this already:

- 2.2, Crumble: *He keeps the ears of half the coast and lends them cheap to
  people he likes.* A man who sells what he hears.
- 2.2, Crumble: *a Maya name in a Spanish port is a thing men collect you for.*
  Leverage, if Cárdenas knows the real name.
- 3.1, Crumble: *Somebody sold the meeting before it happened.*
- 3.1, Crumble: *Domingo stood up out of it so they'd have a man to take.*

Proposed shape, not yet confirmed: Domingo sells Cárdenas small things to stay
uncollected. He sells the meeting. He will not sell the bearing, because Balam
was his friend as a boy. In the trees he gives Crumble the cave and holds the
bearing back, because anything said aloud reaches the clean coat. When the
garrison comes he stands up so Crumble can run. Cárdenas then tortures out of
him the one thing he would not sell.

None of the four lines above needs to change. All four read one way now and
another way after the reveal.

Open:

- Standing informant for years, or squeezed that one afternoon after the spy
  in 2.3 reported a Maya contact. The second keeps the spy sharp.
- Who tells Crumble. Cárdenas telling him, with Domingo in the room, is the
  most in character: he breaks the people around his targets.
- Whether Domingo is alive in the hold.

### The vision, in the hold

Cárdenas doses them with a sacred plant taken from a people he destroyed,
datura or similar, and sits and listens while they talk to their dead. He
learns their wounds the way he learns a people. Proposed, not settled.

Candidates for what each dead man shows, none chosen:

- **Balam** does not tell Crumble he was meant for more. He asks why Crumble
  is still carrying the work. Starts the reframe without finishing it.
- **The father** says his old saying and nothing else. Jigsaw sees where his
  own withholding comes from. Still dead, still not explaining the ring.
- **Bonesy** takes the dose and nothing happens, no body for it to work on. He
  complains he has been left out.

### Cárdenas's magic

Settled: he uses it against the peoples of the Caribbean, and his senior
officers carry artifacts. Rules still to set. Proposals:

- **Every artifact was taken from a people, and works on that people.** A
  Maya piece has a hold on Maya. Crumble is exposed and Bonesy, a cursed Maya
  peasant, most of all. Jigsaw stands outside it until Cárdenas holds
  something of the brotherhood.
- **Settled 2026-10-17: where magic lives.** Magic belongs to the Caribbean
  and the New World. Whether it exists in Europe is never stated. It is
  strongest in native settings, and has passed to pirates through friendship
  and plunder, which is why Tortuga is thick with it. Cárdenas built his
  collection by colonizing South America and then the Maya country.
- **Earlier wording, kept for the record: magic is rare, except in Tortuga.** Tortuga is the hub, a port
  where the strange is common and nobody remarks on it. Aydin's comparison is
  Mos Eisley, with the magic felt more than cast, the way the Force is. Hints,
  not spells: more of the kind of thing the skull at cards in 2.3 already is.
- **Proposed: the reason it is rare elsewhere is him.** He has spent twenty-odd
  years gathering it up the way he gathers books. Tortuga is French and
  lawless, the one place he cannot reach, so the strange pooled there.
- **Each piece costs its carrier.** Small, bodily, visible. Gives Jigsaw
  something to clock in a room, the way he clocked the spy's hands.
- **The bought officer carries one.** The man who took Jigsaw's woman, if he
  matters later. Binds the personal enemy to the magic as well as the codex.

Candidate pieces for Cárdenas himself, all real to the century:

- An obsidian scrying mirror from Mexico. One reached John Dee in England in
  the 1500s.
- A quipu, Inca knotted cord, from the Peru years. He can read it.
- A bezoar stone he drops in his cup against poison.
- Saints' relics and idols on one shelf. The finger came out of his cellar.
- A stone face Bonesy sat with once, now on his shelf. A stone that was taken.
- A second cursed thing, kept in a box. Why he looks at Bonesy the way he does.

Bonesy has already been in one of his cellars at Veracruz, *where the dead ones
were kept* (1.2), so any of this can be paid off by a line from him.

## Under discussion, nothing chosen

### The date: settled at 1670

Aydin moved the book from 1690 to 1670 on 2026-10-11, to put Tortuga at its
height and leave more of the Caribbean unexplored. The outpost in chapter 2 is
now Petit-Goâve. Prose, bible, recap, rules and research are updated. What the
move raised in the prose is in `notes/continuity-flags.md`.

What 1670 gives the book:

- **The hinge year.** The buccaneers are at their height and the law turns on
  them in the same year. England signs the Treaty of Madrid in July 1670 and
  promises Spain to put them down. Morgan sacks Panama six months later anyway.
  This carries what the pardon idea was for.
- **Cárdenas's new posting fits it.** Spain's turn to hunting the brethren is
  the campaign he is just starting.
- **The Itza are free.** An independent Maya kingdom stands in the Petén until
  1697. The thing Cárdenas has spent his life trying to finish is still alive.

Candidate placements for the news of Madrid, none chosen:

- Read off a ship they take. See the merchant raid below.
- An English hand in Cárdenas's pay, a pirate hunter now the crowns agree.
  Henry Morgan's later arc in small.
- Bonesy asking whether a treaty covers a man already dead.

### The merchant raid

Aydin wants Crumble and Jigsaw to take a merchant ship, for the fun of it or
for some reason, with Cárdenas on their tail lending it urgency.

What it can carry:

- **The lift.** `notes/the-crew.md` says the draft is short on the two being
  glad of each other, faster and cockier side by side. This is that scene.
- **Bonesy, what is he doing.** Same note. Something wholly off the job while
  the two of them work.
- **A reason under the fun.** Water and food for a run, a better hull, a chart,
  or news. The news of Madrid fits here.

Placement open. The passage from the cave to the second mark is the natural
home: it needs a scene, it has a reason to resupply, and the urgency is real.
