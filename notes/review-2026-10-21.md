## Response, note by note

### 1. Why is the finger so valuable?

**Where it stands.** The draft says only that a hire wanted the finger of a
small Castilian saint, kept in a gilt silver reliquary, and that Vaz "handles
saints for a living." The outline never says why it is worth so much or who
the hire is. The reader is right that this is a gap.

**Options.**

- **Relic trade alone.** In 1670 a first class relic, a piece of a saint's
  body, could be worth a fortune to a church or a rich family in New Spain.
  Simple and true to the period, but it does no work for the plot.
- **It really works.** Magic is real in this book, so the relic can be too. It
  does one small, real thing: the bone never decays and stops bleeding when
  pressed to a wound, or it is warm to the touch near other magic. That is why
  it was locked in Cárdenas's vault with a label on it, why a collector paid
  a fortune for it, and why Cárdenas comes after the thieves so hard and so
  fast. It turns the first theft into the first move of the war.

**Recommendation.** It really works, kept small. It answers the question, ties
chapter 1 to Cárdenas's collection, and explains his speed at Petit-Goâve
without a coincidence. The hire can stay unnamed for now.

### 2. Backstory in courses, not one buffet

**Where it stands.** This is the strongest note. The outline loads backstory
into three big meals: Jigsaw's night in 4.3, Cárdenas's story in 7.2, and the
vision in 7.3 and 7.4 with 28 numbered steps. That is a buffet.

**Proposal.** Spread it out. Keep the vision for only what each man would
never say aloud. Serve the rest in small courses at quiet moments: a night
watch, a dream, an injury. A first draft of the menu:

**What changes.** The vision shrinks from 28 steps to perhaps 8 or 10 and gets
shorter, perhaps 6,000 words instead of 8,000. Each earlier quiet moment gains
a page. The reader learns them in pieces and keeps wanting the next one.

### 3. Should Cárdenas tell them, or should Bonesy overhear it?

**Where it stands.** Aydin chose on 2026-10-17 that Cárdenas tells his own
history in 7.2. The reader suggests Bonesy, lying still as an ordinary
skeleton, overhears Cárdenas and reports back.

**Why the reader's idea is good.** It is pure Bonesy: nobody looks twice at a
pile of bones. It replaces a villain's speech to prisoners with something
overheard in private, which is more believable and more tense. It also fits
note 2, because it spreads the reveal.

**Where it could go.** In 6.1, before the cave, Bonesy scouts Cárdenas's
camp at the shrine and lies down among the bones of the six men the sea
killed. He hears Cárdenas tell Rodrigo or the elder what the codex really
does and that he accepts the deaths. He gets back to the others, and they go
into the cave anyway, knowing the stakes. The word *before* gets its answer
earlier, from the villain's own mouth.

**The trade off.** Something has to replace 7.2. Suggested: Cárdenas tells
them very little in the cabin. The sister comes out in pieces: her unlabeled
token in 7.1, the glimpse of young Cárdenas with Balam in the vision, the
one piece he uses on Jigsaw at dinner in 8.2. This reverses a decision Aydin
already made, so it is Aydin's call.

### 4. Rodrigo as a minor character

**Where it stands.** Rodrigo appears at the capture (6.3), at one dinner (8.2)
and in his own scene in Florida (9.7). The reader warns against two villains
and suggests he stay minor, standing for something in Jigsaw's life.

**Agreed.** He already stands for the life Jigsaw thinks was stolen. The risk
is 9.7, a full scene that makes him feel like a second boss fight. Suggestion:
fold 9.7 into the smaller fights. Rodrigo is in the way, Jigsaw beats him in a
few lines, and walks past him without looking back. The point lands harder if
it is quick: Rodrigo is no longer worth a scene to him. The reader's Kurtz in
the cave idea is set aside, as the reader suggests.

### 5. How deep the backstory goes

**Agreed.** Note 2 covers when. On how much: the outline already skips how they
learned their skills, and the bible's detail on that (the underground duels,
the first captain, the solo years) can stay in the bible as the writer's
knowledge, not the reader's. Familiar shapes do some of the lifting: the
teacher who died with the work undone, the classmate who got the commission,
the girl who married the other man. Readers fill those in from other stories.

### 6. What kind of villain

**The choice the reader asks for.** Cárdenas is already closest to the
representative villain, the Kurtz: he is what the two men could become.

- **For Jigsaw**, he is the man whose anger and need for control won. Both are
  clever, angry, hate being powerless and never submit. The fort chapter
  already says this.
- **For Crumble**, he is not yet tied in enough. The brainstorm of 2026-10-18
  has the fix, idea 2: Cárdenas was also Balam's student, overhears that
  Balam called him his greatest mistake, and never stops trying to prove Balam
  wrong. Crumble learns to stop needing Balam's approval. Cárdenas never does.
  That makes him a mirror for both men.

The self as villain is present too, and should stay a layer, not the main
choice: Jigsaw's rage at the merchant ship and the patrol, and Crumble's
debt to Balam, are what actually lose them the fight in chapter 6.

**Recommendation.** State it plainly in the outline: Cárdenas is the
representative villain. Then adopt brainstorm idea 2 so he reflects Crumble as
well as Jigsaw.

### 7. Start the quest right after the finger

**Where it stands.** The quest starts at the end of 1.2, when Bonesy produces
the finger. Chapter 2 is selling it, outfitting at Petit-Goâve, the spy and
Domingo, about 4,800 words before they sail for the cay.

**What can tighten.** The finger sale, the spy and Domingo all do plot work
and should stay. The fat is in 2.2, which the scene index describes as "their
own gear described at length." Trimming 2.2 hard, and folding 2.1 (351 words)
into the end of 1.2 or the start of 2.2, would get them to the escape faster.
That is a line editing job on drafted prose, so it waits on Aydin's go ahead.
