# The back half, from Aydin's notes of 2026-10-16

A deep-dive on Aydin's notes from the story doc, fleshed out with suggestions.
It replaces `notes/back-half-arena.md` wherever the two differ. The doc
*Book One: The Story* carries the short version scene by scene. This file keeps
the detail. Everything marked *invented* or *suggested* is open to veto.

## The clue chain

**Clue one, the leaf map (4.6).** Painted Mesoamerican maps can put east at the
top and mark it red; roads are footprints; numbers are bars (five) and dots
(one). Nobody can make the painted coast a coast until Jigsaw turns the leaf red
edge up. Then it is the shore north of their island, with canoe-day counts
between landmarks, about fourteen days north to a long island with the moon
lady's sign: Cozumel, Ixchel's pilgrim island. Beside her sign is a glyph Crumble
cannot read.

**Clue two, the glyph (5.4).** The women's sign for the Lady singing (*invented*).
Balam's daughter: a small shrine on Cozumel's south point has openings that make
it whistle in a north wind (real: El Caracol). The leaf says the Lady's house is
where she sings, and the door is open when she sings. So the second mark is a sea
cave under the shrine that uncovers only in a norther's surge. Cárdenas's men
forced it in calm with powder and the sea killed six.

**Clue three, the second leaf (8.2).** A Venus page like the Dresden Codex table:
the great star goes under and returns in the east after about eight days as
morning star. Stand at the Lady's house the morning he first returns and sail at
where he rises for a count of days. Cárdenas reads every glyph but cannot know
which morning: that takes an unbroken living day count, and he has spent forty
years killing day keepers. Domingo is broken or dead. His own spy lost the count
as a boy in a Spanish parish. Crumble has said the day name under his breath every
dawn since Balam died (*invented*, needs two seeded lines in 3.1 and 4.1). Jigsaw
gives the bearing of the rising and turns canoe days along a swell into a ship's
leagues.

**The third mark.** East of south from Cozumel, open sea, a drowned cay.
Suggested: the Swan Islands, uninhabited in 1670.

## Tipu, the Maya town

Tipu on the Belize River, in revolt from 1638 and free in 1670, looking to the
Itza at Nojpetén. The strange survives there because Cárdenas has not reached it:
he gathers magic from conquered peoples the way he gathers books, so it pools
where he has not been. Tortuga is French, and Tipu is forest. Rules, kept light: a
cursed piece works on the people it was taken from, and costs its holder something
small and bodily, a tremor, a cough, white in one eye. The house of old things
holds a jade that locks the limbs, a conch nobody blows, and a bundle of leaves
that are not the codex. Nobody casts. They keep.

## Balam's daughter

Suggested names: **Kan**, a day name, with a Spanish port name Catalina; or
**Nikte'**, flower. Avoid an Ix- name on the page so she does not echo Ixmal. She
keeps the old things and the women's reading of signs, can sail a dugout, and can
keep a day count. Balam split his teaching between her and Crumble, the way the
priests split the book. Love in small behavior: she takes the tiller and he lets
her; she mends the red sash; she uses Jigsaw's hand sign for a reef before Crumble
does; Crumble stops saying it is fine around her. Captured at 6.3, held in the
women's cell at Campeche, released at 8.2.

## The spy

A grey Maya, suggested name Ah Pech, Spanish name Pablo. Raised in a Spanish parish
and bought long ago. He knows the Balam stories because Cárdenas collected them,
and he uses the one key Crumble never guards: anyone who was Balam's is trusted. He
hears the reading of the door and the norther and sends word by canoe. Domingo
gave Cárdenas the island. The spy gives him the door and the weather. At 6.3 he
takes Crumble by holding a knife to Balam's daughter.

## Cárdenas at the second mark

For Jigsaw: buoys on his reef, a ship in his anchorage, and the shrine's song
blown on Cárdenas's own whistle, a copy his masons cut. For Crumble: his glyphs
corrected in better Yucatec, Balam quoted, and a look in the obsidian mirror, where
he sees Balam at a Spanish table. The mirror shows each man one true still picture.
Jigsaw sees his pool with no tarpon in it.

The schoolmate officer, suggested **Don Rodrigo de Ayala**, should be the bought
officer who married Jigsaw's girl. That lands the bible's reveal and gives 6.3
and 9.2 a face. To soften the coincidence: Cárdenas took him on because he knows
Jigsaw. Make him good, a real swordsman who has studied Jigsaw's left-hand feint
for eight years, decent in manner, which is worse.

## Cárdenas's speech, before the dose

Courteous, short, no heat.

> "The book is a key. Made whole it takes the strange out of these seas. Your
> friend. The pool. The thing in your mother's blood, señor. All of it. After
> that the islands are ordered the way God and the king intend. A man will not
> be free to be odd. He will have a place and he will keep it. I am telling you
> so you understand what you have been working for. Not treasure. The last door
> in the world that lets a young man be what he is. I would like to know what
> two such men dream of. Drink."

Check: "your mother's blood" should be "your father's blood". The corsair line in
the bible comes through Jigsaw's father.

## The shared vision

Ololiuhqui seed, banned by the Inquisition in New Spain from 1620. Bonesy appears
as a man: short, bow-legged, a gap in his teeth, corn-ground hands, "Hello,
fellas." He herds them through their pasts like goats.

- **Jigsaw sees of Crumble** (*invented*): on the raid Crumble killed a crewmate
  to stop him at Balam's daughter, his first kill of his own choosing, which is
  why he left that crew. At Ixmal's door the husband gave him water, called him
  señor, and was kind.
- **Crumble sees of Jigsaw** (*invented*): the boy at a rich classmate's kitchen
  door, waiting with the servants for the price of the cure, told no, and doing
  nothing. The rage is at himself first. And the underground duels where he
  nearly killed a boy for a look.
- **The father:** the corsair blade wrapped and put under the floor, the boy
  taught the water from a plantation shore, a hand on the sleeping boy's head and
  the saying. Nothing said.
- **Balam:** at a young Cárdenas's table, giving up a free town's hiding place for
  his little daughter's life. He turns to Crumble and does not apologize. "You
  left a man on his knees for the two in the boat. Good." Crumble has already
  done Balam's sin at Petit-Goâve, and is told it was right.

## Flags

1. **The escape and the fort.** The notes say the escape is easy, then that they
   are kept in a fort. Resolution: the escape happens inside the vision. The dose
   was meant to break them, and they come out of it stronger. They wake in irons
   bound for Campeche, and there is no physical escape until the release.
2. **Book one ends at the third mark,** in a standoff: three leaves, two of them
   his. The codex gate at the end of book three still stands.
3. **Domingo in 3.1** "stood up so they'd have a man to take." Either change the
   line, or let Crumble have told it kinder than it was, and Cárdenas corrects it
   in 7.1. Suggested: Domingo dies in the hold.
4. **Magic rare except in Tortuga.** Tipu is a second pocket. The rule that magic
   survives where Cárdenas has not been reconciles them. The bible needs a line
   on it.
5. **Balam's trade** means Cárdenas has known about the daughter for twenty
   years. Her capture is a long game, and his manner with her should show it.
6. **Crumble's day count** needs seeding, or it reads as convenient.
7. **The pool paragraph in 4.1** is already flagged. The alone-then, together-now
   contrast in 4.3 could replace it.
8. **Campeche's fort** in 1670: confirm what stood there before the great walls
   of the 1680s.
