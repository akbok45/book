# The back half, from Aydin's notes of 2026-10-16

A deep-dive on Aydin's notes from the story doc, fleshed out with suggestions.
It replaces `notes/back-half-arena.md` wherever the two differ. The doc
*Book One: The Story* carries the short version scene by scene. This file keeps
the detail. Everything marked *invented* or *suggested* is open to veto.

## The clue chain

**Clue one, the leaf map (4.6).** Painted Mesoamerican maps can put east at the
top and mark it red; roads are footprints; numbers are bars (five) and dots
(one). Nobody can make the painted coast a coast until Jigsaw turns the leaf red
edge up. Then it is the shore north of their island, with canoe-day counts
between landmarks, about fourteen days north to a long island with the moon
lady's sign: Cozumel, Ixchel's pilgrim island. Beside her sign is a glyph Crumble
cannot read.

**Clue two, the glyph (5.4).** The women's sign for the Lady singing (*invented*).
Balam's daughter: a small shrine on Cozumel's south point has openings that make
it whistle in a north wind (real: El Caracol). The leaf says the Lady's house is
where she sings, and the door is open when she sings. So the second mark is a sea
cave under the shrine that uncovers only in a norther's surge. Cárdenas's men
forced it in calm with powder and the sea killed six.

**Clue three, the second leaf (8.2).** A Venus page like the Dresden Codex table:
the great star goes under and returns in the east after about eight days as
morning star. Stand at the Lady's house the morning he first returns and sail at
where he rises for a count of days. Cárdenas reads every glyph but cannot know
which morning: that takes an unbroken living day count, and he has spent twenty-odd
years killing day keepers. Domingo is broken or dead. His own spy lost the count
as a boy in a Spanish parish. Crumble has said the day name under his breath every
dawn since Balam died (*invented*, needs two seeded lines in 3.1 and 4.1). Jigsaw
gives the bearing of the rising and turns canoe days along a swell into a ship's
leagues.

**The third mark.** East of south from Cozumel, open sea, a drowned cay.
Suggested: the Swan Islands, uninhabited in 1670.

## Tipu, the Maya town

Tipu on the Belize River, in revolt from 1638 and free in 1670, looking to the
Itza at Nojpetén. The strange survives there because Cárdenas has not reached it:
he gathers magic from conquered peoples the way he gathers books, so it pools
where he has not been. Tortuga is French, and Tipu is forest. Rules, kept light: a
cursed piece works on the people it was taken from, and costs its holder something
small and bodily, a tremor, a cough, white in one eye. The house of old things
holds a jade that locks the limbs, a conch nobody blows, and a bundle of leaves
that are not the codex. Nobody casts. They keep.

## Balam's daughter

Suggested names: **Kan**, a day name, with a Spanish port name Catalina; or
**Nikte'**, flower. Avoid an Ix- name on the page so she does not echo Ixmal. She
keeps the old things and the women's reading of signs, can sail a dugout, and can
keep a day count. Balam split his teaching between her and Crumble, the way the
priests split the book. Love in small behavior: she takes the tiller and he lets
her; she mends the red sash; she uses Jigsaw's hand sign for a reef before Crumble
does; Crumble stops saying it is fine around her. Captured at 6.3, held in the
women's cell at Campeche, released at 8.2.

## The spy

A grey Maya, suggested name Ah Pech, Spanish name Pablo. Raised in a Spanish parish
and bought long ago. He knows the Balam stories because Cárdenas collected them,
and he uses the one key Crumble never guards: anyone who was Balam's is trusted. He
hears the reading of the door and the norther and sends word by canoe. Domingo
gave Cárdenas the island. The spy gives him the door and the weather. At 6.3 he
takes Crumble by holding a knife to Balam's daughter.

## Cárdenas at the second mark

For Jigsaw: buoys on his reef, a ship in his anchorage, and the shrine's song
blown on Cárdenas's own whistle, a copy his masons cut. For Crumble: his glyphs
corrected in better Yucatec, Balam quoted, and a look in the obsidian mirror, where
he sees Balam at a Spanish table. The mirror shows each man one true still picture.
Jigsaw sees his pool with no tarpon in it.

The schoolmate officer, suggested **Don Rodrigo de Ayala**, should be the bought
officer who married Jigsaw's girl. That lands the bible's reveal and gives 6.3
and 9.2 a face. To soften the coincidence: Cárdenas took him on because he knows
Jigsaw. Make him good, a real swordsman who has studied Jigsaw's left-hand feint
for eight years, decent in manner, which is worse.

## Cárdenas's speech, before the dose

Courteous, short, no heat.

> "The book is a key. Made whole it takes the strange out of these seas. Your
> friend. The pool. The thing in your mother's blood, señor. All of it. After
> that the islands are ordered the way God and the king intend. A man will not
> be free to be odd. He will have a place and he will keep it. I am telling you
> so you understand what you have been working for. Not treasure. The last door
> in the world that lets a young man be what he is. I would like to know what
> two such men dream of. Drink."

Check: "your mother's blood" should be "your father's blood". The corsair line in
the bible comes through Jigsaw's father.

## The shared vision

Ololiuhqui seed, banned by the Inquisition in New Spain from 1620. Bonesy appears
as a man: short, bow-legged, a gap in his teeth, corn-ground hands, "Hello,
fellas." He herds them through their pasts like goats.

- **Jigsaw sees of Crumble** (*invented*): on the raid Crumble killed a crewmate
  to stop him at Balam's daughter, his first kill of his own choosing, which is
  why he left that crew. At Ixmal's door the husband gave him water, called him
  señor, and was kind.
- **Crumble sees of Jigsaw** (*invented*): the boy at a rich classmate's kitchen
  door, waiting with the servants for the price of the cure, told no, and doing
  nothing. The rage is at himself first. And the underground duels where he
  nearly killed a boy for a look.
- **The father:** the corsair blade wrapped and put under the floor, the boy
  taught the water from a plantation shore, a hand on the sleeping boy's head and
  the saying. Nothing said.
- **Balam:** at a young Cárdenas's table, giving up a free town's hiding place for
  his little daughter's life. He turns to Crumble and does not apologize. "You
  left a man on his knees for the two in the boat. Good." Crumble has already
  done Balam's sin at Petit-Goâve, and is told it was right.

## Flags

1. **The escape and the fort.** The notes say the escape is easy, then that they
   are kept in a fort. Resolution: the escape happens inside the vision. The dose
   was meant to break them, and they come out of it stronger. They wake in irons
   bound for Campeche, and there is no physical escape until the release.
2. **Book one ends at the third mark,** in a standoff: three leaves, two of them
   his. The codex gate at the end of book three still stands.
3. **Domingo in 3.1** "stood up so they'd have a man to take." Either change the
   line, or let Crumble have told it kinder than it was, and Cárdenas corrects it
   in 7.1. Suggested: Domingo dies in the hold.
4. **Magic rare except in Tortuga.** Tipu is a second pocket. The rule that magic
   survives where Cárdenas has not been reconciles them. The bible needs a line
   on it.
5. **Balam's trade** means Cárdenas has known about the daughter for twenty
   years. Her capture is a long game, and his manner with her should show it.
6. **Crumble's day count** needs seeding, or it reads as convenient.
7. **The pool paragraph in 4.1** is already flagged. The alone-then, together-now
   contrast in 4.3 could replace it.
8. **Campeche's fort** in 1670: confirm what stood there before the great walls
   of the 1680s.

## Aydin's second pass, 2026-10-16

These override the sections above where they differ. The story doc carries the
result scene by scene.

- **No Ixchel in the clue chain.** The leaf points to Cozumel. The second glyph
  names a shrine that whistles in a north wind, and the door opens only when
  the shrine sings.
- **6.1, their flaws trap them.** Jigsaw's confidence in his sea takes them
  past a point into a channel Cárdenas's pilots know better. Crumble's trust in
  anything of Balam's let the spy in.
- **6.2, the mirror.** Each brother sees his best memory: Crumble training with
  Balam, Jigsaw's father first showing him the sea. Cárdenas walks into each and
  kills the idol.
- **6.3, the captures.** Rodrigo rose on his father's connections and taunts
  Jigsaw as he beats him. Crumble loses to a Maya fighter trained by Cárdenas in
  techniques Cárdenas learned from Balam.
- **7.1, the cabin.** Worldbuilding of the cabin. Cárdenas explains artifacts he
  used to study them and artifacts he used to hurt people. A cold line with the
  drink. They resist, with swords at their throats.
- **Cárdenas gets a sad past** that makes him almost sympathetic. Proposed: a
  little sister lost to a curse on a relic his father looted in Peru.
- **7.2 and 7.3, the vision.** Crumble sees Jigsaw's rage on a merchant deck,
  Jigsaw disarming Rodrigo, Jigsaw happy with the girl and then the girl with
  Rodrigo, Jigsaw sailing with his father, and the father as a Barbary corsair
  making a deal with the Spanish to raise his son safely. Jigsaw sees Balam
  teach a young Cárdenas out of kindness, then hide when he saw what Cárdenas
  became, kind to Cárdenas and harsh to Crumble, and Crumble at Ixmal's door.
  Each then asks his father or mentor why.
- **Bonesy is left on the island in the chest.**
- **8, the fort.** What they learned from their fathers works on them. They
  spar with sticks, and Balam's daughter trains them. Jigsaw sees Crumble's love
  for her and steps aside, though he likes her too. His father's words turn him
  toward the deal, to save Bonesy, who is family.
- **Cárdenas does not tell them where Bonesy is.** They know. He expects them
  to go home broken, and he is right until Bonesy rallies them with what he saw
  in their visions: that was your fathers' choice, now make yours. Later Bonesy
  admits he was manipulating them, because if Cárdenas wins he dies.
- **The deal hands Cárdenas the last location,** a very deep temple in a south
  Florida estuary that is hard for him to navigate, which buys time. The
  brothers meet him there, learn a new land together, and fight him again after
  a second gearing-up scene. This does not end the book. It leads into a final
  battle sequence, to be built next.

## Aydin's third pass, 2026-10-16

These override the sections above where they differ.

- **4.3** focuses on his parents' death and arriving in these waters after
  leaving the navy, completely alone.
- **5.1 is a flashback raid,** not the coaster. It shows Jigsaw's rage and
  Crumble's faith in Balam's rules. The news of Madrid now arrives with a
  trader at the village.
- **The village is an island,** in Aydin's words. Proposed: a small island in a
  forest lake up a river, replacing Tipu, which is a river town. It is a happy
  farming community of cursed and different people who put their curses to
  creative use. It is not a place of dark magic.
- **The spy is deep undercover:** a respected elder of that island who traded
  his loyalty to Cárdenas so the Spanish would leave the island alone. He takes
  Crumble down at the capture.
- **Balam's daughter insists on coming and leads.** She wants the codex kept from
  Cárdenas because it would unmake her people, not because of her father. Her

- **6.1:** Cárdenas uses an artifact to turn the current, and Jigsaw doesn't
  notice.
- **6.3:** Rodrigo had worse marks but rose above Jigsaw in the navy on family

- **Domingo is let go after the cabin.** Cárdenas doesn't kill when breaking will

- **Before the deal:** a flashback to Tortuga and Jigsaw's non-human friends

## Fourth pass, 2026-10-16: the outside review

Aydin brought in an outside review (`notes/review-2026-10-16.md`) and asked for
all of it to be built on top of the existing road. The story doc now carries:

- **The heart of the book.** Both men mistake independence for freedom. The
  final battle tests whether they became different men, not better fighters.
- **Cárdenas fears uncertainty, not magic.** His sister died while experts
  argued. Knowledge became collection, collection became classification,
  classification became control. The cabin is that worldview on shelves, and
  Bonesy is the one thing he can't shelve.
- **Balam stays unresolved.** "I regret what he became. I don't regret saving
  her." He split his teaching on purpose, and the split pays off in Florida.
- **The cord style takes two people,** one binding and one striking. Crumble
  knows one half and Balam's daughter the other. Jigsaw learns to fight with
  them.
- **Balam's daughter's real flaw:** she knows what everyone needs except
  herself. Her first want is small and strange.
- **Rodrigo stands for the stolen life.** The girl's side is complicated by
  Jigsaw's own anger. In Florida Jigsaw beats him and stops caring.
- **The raid flashback is ugly.** **The village's curses are inconvenient,**
  not superpowers. **Domingo complicates the fathers' lesson.** **The fort
  starts in misery,** with Cárdenas allowing the sticks. **Keeping his word is
  his strongest moment.** **The Bonesy rescue mirrors chapter 3.**
- **The codex rules, proposed.** It restores rather than destroys. Whole means
  leaves, day count and rite together at the right place and day. Florida is
  where it is used. It can be broken again.
- **Three battle versions,** with Cárdenas losing to his own need for control,
  surviving, and the codex broken again. The epilogue at Tortuga brings the
  news of Morgan.

## Fifth pass, 2026-10-17: the full plain English revision

Aydin asked for the whole story doc to be rewritten, not added to, in simple,
direct English, with every scene saying what happens, why, and what changes.
The doc was rewritten section by section. What changed from the fourth pass:

- **Magic is rare and mostly story.** People see pieces of it now and then.
  It survives in isolated native communities. Tortuga has more only because it
  takes in anyone. Amara's community is where it is strongest.
- **The codex follows impersonal rules.** The bond between the three is no
  longer the key. Five requirements: leaves, living day count, rite, place,
  time. A flawed rite works slowly.
- **Cárdenas hates magic** and uses only what he controls. His sister died of
  magic nobody could control. He wants magic gone from the Caribbean and
  accepts the deaths the codex would cause. He prefers breaking enemies to
  killing them but still kills. "He never chases" is cut. His history with
  Balam is laid out, and he tells most of it himself in chapter 7.
- **Balam's line** is now "I regret what he did with what I taught him. I
  could not know what he would become." His deals with Spain to protect his
  people and Amara are a separate choice.
- **Anne is final.** Crumble left because he thought he was not enough for
  her, and repeats the pattern with Balam.
- **Amara** is the daughter's name. Fully human, a leader of her own people,
  flaw is not knowing what she wants.
- **The village** uses old, physical changes (mangrove legs, a shark-like
  fisherman, a crab claw, coral growth, bark) with at least one tragic case.
  The in-world word for these people is still open.
- **Jigsaw's deeper need** is proof he mattered. His arc is choosing what is
  worth something, as his father did. The school duel shows him attacking
  after the fight is called.
- **Rodrigo** is capable, and the resentment runs both ways.
- **Domingo** is an acquaintance of Balam. His role ends at his torture. The
  prison visit and the "my people" line are cut.
- **Chapter 4** is the first success, not the midpoint. Jigsaw is afraid and
  goes in. The clue leads to Amara, who can read it and also hide them.
- **Chapter 5** has no standalone flashback. A Spanish patrol does six jobs:
  introduces Amara as a leader, gives action, triggers Jigsaw's raid memory,
  tests whether he stops, reveals the Treaty of Madrid, and shows Spanish
  pressure reaching the village, with a followable chain.
- **Chapter 6 capture:** the elder beats Crumble with Balam's own methods,
  soldiers overpower Amara, Rodrigo threatens Crumble, and Jigsaw surrenders.
  This is the midpoint.
- **Chapter 7:** the cabin, Cárdenas's story, then a chronological vision with
  a 12 step list for Jigsaw and a 16 step list for Crumble, then the two
  conversations, then Bonesy's chest.
- **The cord subplot is cut.** Chapter 8 is Cárdenas trying to break Jigsaw
  with privileges, then freedom alone (refused), then the genuine deal. No
  weapons asked for. The ugly rescue, then the beach.
- **Chapter 9** is a 14 part Florida climax of about 20,000 words. Cárdenas
  loses because he cannot hand anything essential to anyone. Jigsaw leaves
  Rodrigo alive. No epilogue. Morgan waits.
- **Battle versions A, B and C** are folded into one climax, with a note on
  what came from each.
