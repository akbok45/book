## 2. The clue chain

**Clue one, the leaf map (4.6).** Maya painted maps put east at the top and mark east red; roads are footprints; numbers are bars (five) and dots (one). Crumble says all three facts in a line each. The puzzle is that nobody can make the painted coast a coast until Jigsaw turns the leaf red edge up, and then it is the shore north of their island, footprints along it with day counts between landmarks, canoe days for a coasting trader. He sums them to about fourteen days north to a long island with a moon lady sign. Cozumel, Ixchel's pilgrim island. Beside her sign a second glyph Crumble cannot read.

**Clue two, the glyph (5.4).** The glyph is the women's sign for the Lady singing (*invented*). Kan: on Cozumel there is a small shrine on the south point with openings cut so a north wind makes it whistle (real, El Caracol). The leaf says the Lady's house is where she sings, and the door is open when she sings. So the second mark is a sea cave under that shrine that only uncovers in a norther's surge. Cárdenas's men forced it in calm with powder and the sea killed six. Hobbit shape: a place named by a sound, a door that opens on one weather.

**Clue three, the second leaf, which Cárdenas cannot solve.** It is a Venus page like the Dresden table: the great star goes under and comes back in the east after eight days as morning star. The leaf says to stand at the Lady's house on the morning he first returns and sail at where he rises for a count of days. Cárdenas reads every glyph. He cannot know which morning, because that takes a living day count carried unbroken, and he has spent forty years killing day keepers. Domingo is dead. His own Maya spy was raised in a Spanish parish and lost the count as a boy. Crumble has said the day name under his breath every dawn since Balam died (*invented*; two lines could seed it in 3.1 and 4.1). The count gives the morning; Jigsaw gives the azimuth of the rising and turns the days into leagues for a canoe in that season's wind. Cárdenas's pilots cannot, because the leaf's count is in canoe days along a swell, not a ship's leagues. That is why he needs both men and not one.

**Pointer to the third mark.** East of south from Cozumel, open sea, a drowned cay. Suggest the Swan Islands (Santanilla), uninhabited in 1670; Aydin's call.

## 3. The Maya village

Tipu, on the Belize River, a real Maya town in revolt from 1638 and free in 1670, looking to the Itza king at Nojpetén. Reached by a few days up a brown river under trees. The strange survives there because Cárdenas has not reached it: he gathered magic from conquered peoples the way he gathers books, so it pools where he has not been, Tortuga because it is French, Tipu because it is forest. Rules, kept light: a thing works on the people it was taken from, and costs its holder something small and bodily, a tremor, a cough, white in one eye. Jigsaw clocks these the way he clocked the spy's hands. The house of old things: a jade that locks the limbs, a conch nobody blows, a bundle of leaves that are not the codex. Nobody there casts anything. They keep.

Jigsaw inland: no horizon, so he keeps looking for one. Still air he cannot read. He walks with a hand on his hilt in a town that means him no harm. Crumble steadies him by being ahead and looking back, and by speaking Maya softly to a child, which tells Jigsaw the room is safe before he can read it himself.

## 4. Balam's daughter

Name suggestions: **Ix Kan** (Kan, a day name, period plausible for a woman), Spanish name Catalina in port, the double name rhyming with Domingo. Or **Nikte'**, flower. Her skills: keeper of the old things and of the women's reading of signs, the moon count, the river. She can sail a dugout and she can keep a day count, which matters later.

They find her because Crumble knows Balam sent her to her mother's people at Tipu, and only she can read the glyph. She knows him from the raid, three years back, and says nothing until he does. Love in small behavior: she takes the tiller and he lets her; she mends the red sash; he teaches her Jigsaw's hand sign for a reef and she uses it before he does; he stops saying it is fine around her, and Jigsaw notices the silence change shape. Bonesy pries, she answers plain, and he is undone.

Captured at 6.3 by the guide's knife, held in the women's cell at Campeche, released with the men at 8.2. The rescue that pulls Crumble is therefore in chapter 8, hers, and Bonesy's in 8.3.

## 5. The spy
