# The village arc: development pass

2026-10-28. This answers Aydin's brief "The Village Arc: Comprehensive Story
Development Brief." It does the seven steps the brief asks for:
1. continuity constraints;
2. a chapter structure;
3. the ceremony in depth;
4. weaknesses;
5. comparisons where the best choice is not obvious;
6. mystery kept;
7. story before explanation.

Nothing here is drafted prose and nothing is final. Anything marked
*invented* is open to veto. Anything marked **decide** is a question only
Aydin can answer.

---

## 1. Continuity check: what the brief collides with

These are the places where the brief and the existing book disagree. Each needs
a ruling before drafting. They are ordered by how much they move.

### Hard collisions

1. **The year.** The brief says "set around 1690." CLAUDE.md and every drafted
   scene say 1670, and the Treaty of Madrid letter depends on it. I have kept
   1670. **Decide:** confirm 1670.

2. **Human Bonesy is already spent in chapter 7.** Outline 7.3: "Bonesy cannot
   be drugged, but he appears in the vision anyway, as the man he was: short,
   bow legged and missing teeth. 'Hello, fellas.'" The brief makes the
   ceremony the first time the brothers see him alive, so the 7.3 beat has to
   go. **Proposal:** the ceremony gets the first sight of him. In 7.3 he does
   not appear at all; he is in the chest, and his absence is the point.

3. **"Balam appears once"** (settled 2026-10-17, in the chapter 7 vision). The
   ceremony now shows Balam twice: teaching the student and fighting the
   massacre. **Proposal:** treat these as the past replaying, not Balam
   appearing. He never sees or speaks to the three. The chapter 7
   conversation, where he answers Crumble, stays his one real appearance.
   **Decide.**

4. **Chapter 7.4 already shows "a short look at Balam and young Cárdenas" and
   "Balam realizing what Cárdenas has become and cutting him off."** The
   ceremony now owns that material, with the face hidden. **Proposal:** cut
   both bullets from 7.4. Keep Crumble's 7.4 conversation with Balam, but
   change his question from "why did you teach Cárdenas" to the one the
   ceremony leaves him with: "why didn't you teach me."

5. **The jade lock in 6.3.** "Cárdenas locks him rigid with a jade piece from
   his collection." The brief replaces this with the black bell, which
   collapses Bonesy at the capture. **Proposal:** in 6.3 the bell replaces the
   jade, and the jade comes out of the 7.1 shelf list. **Decide** whether the
   bell rings in 6.3 (the capture) or in 7.1 (the cabin, once they are
   chained).

6. **How Cárdenas met Balam (7.2).** The outline has Cárdenas winning Balam's
   trust by stopping soldiers burning a Maya settlement. The brief has him
   bringing soldiers to burn Balam's own village. Both can be true, and the
   two together are worse than either alone. **Proposal:** he spared the
   settlement to win Balam's trust, and years later he came back with the
   soldiers and burned it. That fits the brief's "the villagers recognize him"
   exactly: he is the officer who once saved them. **Decide.**

7. **Who learns what, and when.** Outline 7.2 has Cárdenas confess his history
   to Bonesy alone. The brief has the identity and the bell revealed at the
   capture, to everyone. **Proposal:** split it.
   - At the capture, the bell rings, and Crumble recognizes the stillness from
     the vision.
   - In the cabin (7.1), Cárdenas questions Crumble about what Balam taught
     him. He is amused that Balam never taught Crumble magic, and in being
     amused he gives himself away. "He taught me first."
   - In 7.2, alone with Bonesy, he gives the sister and what the codex does.
     That is the brief's "separate dramatic revelation."

8. **Crumble's silence after the bend.** In the drafted 5.4 and 5.5 Crumble
   says nothing, matching the bible: "Nobody said a word." The brief has
   Crumble confront Jigsaw, without abandoning him. **Proposal:** the
   merchant ship two years ago is the time nobody said a word. This time
   Crumble does. Breaking the silence is the change.

9. **The codex's function, in words.** The outline says the codex "restores
   everything magic has changed to what it was before." The brief says it
   "destroys all magic and curses," killing whoever depends on magic. These
   are the same effect. "Restore" is how it works; "the end of magic" is what
   it does to the world. **Proposal:** keep "restore" as the word Cárdenas
   uses, because it sounds like mercy. That is how he persuades people. The
   reader learns in 7.2 that restore means extinction. **Decide** the term.

10. **Outline 5.7 says Balam told Amara the codex would end her people.** The
    brief says Amara does not know. **Proposal:** cut that line. Amara comes
    for her own reasons (see 5.9 below).

### Softer collisions

- **The bible's "practical deals with Spain to protect his people."** This now
  sits badly with Spain burning his village. **Proposal:** the deals came
  after the burning, and they are why Ah Pech later thinks dealing with Spain
  is survival. He learned it from Balam.
- **Ah Pech is missing from the brief.** He fought beside Balam, he trades
  information to Cárdenas, and he beats Crumble in 6.3. He has to be in this
  arc. **Option, strong, *invented*:** Ah Pech is a survivor of Balam's old
  village. He saw the stillness. He knows who the student was. His deal with
  Cárdenas is not greed but fear of a man who can switch your people off.
  The ceremony then hurts him to watch. **Decide.**
- **Amara's age and the massacre.** She is 20 or 21, and the brief says the
  massacre came "before her time." Cárdenas is in his early forties. Teaching
  him in his twenties and burning the village a few years later puts the
  massacre roughly 18 to 22 years back, around Amara's birth. **Option:** she
  was a baby carried out of the fire. Her mother died there. Amara has no
  memory of it, which is why it is "before her time." **Decide** whether her
  mother died in the massacre.
- **The leaf's age.** The leaf was sealed in the jar by the old priests,
  centuries ago. It is not in Balam's hand. Amara's recognition has to come
  from somewhere else (see comparison B).
- **The apothecary was just used.** 5.1 already has the apothecary dream, which
  is Aydin's final text. The ceremony version must be short and changed, not
  a rerun: the drawer, the leaf, "It isn't yours," and blood.
- **Powers.** The bible says changes are "old, physical and sometimes
  frightening, never cute powers." The brief's massacre has a woman calling
  wind and a man of great strength. **Proposal:** keep abilities bodily and
  costly, like the island's. A woman whose breath can push a canoe, who is
  winded for a day after. A man with a turtle's back who stands in a doorway.
- **The "coat."** The brief has the student reach "beneath his coat." A coat
  on a man in a Maya village points straight at a Spaniard. **Proposal:** a
  plain cloak, or a cloth worn the way the villagers wear it. The reader must
  not be able to guess from the cut of his clothes.
- **The patrol's spotting.** The root man's sensing replaces the two boys in
  the drafted 5.3. The drafted scouting and the rain plan can stay, retimed.
- **Bonesy's past.** The brief makes him a farmer. Chapter 7 has him
  remembering "mostly barrels," and chapter 1 has him overhearing priests in
  their cellars. **Option, *invented*:** he was a farmer who carried jars of
  honey and balché down to the priests' cellars, which is how he heard them.
  The farmer and the barrels are one man. **Decide.**
- **Length and numbering.** See the structure below.

## 3. The ceremony in depth

### The rules

The ceremony needs rules, or it becomes a source of unlimited answers.

1. **The road goes where your dead are.** *Invented.* The drink opens the way
   for the living to walk among the dead, and each living walker is drawn
   toward the dead he carries.
   - **Crumble** carries Balam, so the road runs through Balam's past.
   - **Jigsaw** carries his parents, so it comes round to the shop.
   - **Bonesy** is dead already, so the road begins in his own field.
   - That is why it shows these things and nothing else. Nobody can ask it
     for the codex's secret.
2. **You see, you do not touch.** The past cannot be changed or questioned.
   Blades pass through, and hands do not hold.
3. **Speech.** On the road, speech is plain to anyone who walks it. Jigsaw
   understands Balam and the student without knowing Maya. *Invented.* The
   alternative is Crumble whispering translations, which slows the burning to
   a crawl.
4. **The clock is the lilies.** The water lilies open in the morning. Come
   back before they open, or you do not come back. *Real basis: Nymphaea
   ampla, the Maya water lily, opens by day.* This gives the burning and the
   drawer a deadline, and it gives Bonesy a reason to drag Jigsaw out.
5. **Why Bonesy can guide.** He has already crossed. He does not drink, because
   the drink cannot touch him. He lies down in the canoe between them, and
   when they go, he goes. On the road he can see where the living cannot, as
   he sees in the dark (5.1, established). He knows the way the way he knew
   the coral, by feel. He does not understand it, and he says so.

### What is real and what is invented

| Element | Real basis | Invented |
|---|---|---|
| Balché | Fermented honey and balché bark, a Maya ritual drink | Its strength here |
| Water lily | Maya art ties the lily to the underworld water | Steeping it in the drink |
| The four coloured roads | The Popol Vuh, at the crossroads of Xibalba | That Crumble learned them from Balam |
| The fifth direction | The Maya centre, green, the world tree | A road down through it |
| The drum | Maya turtle shell drums | The claw woman playing one |
| Canoes on the lake | none | The whole setting |

The real practices still need checking before drafting (section 6).

### Bonesy's field (6.2)

- **Waking.** They wake at dawn in a maize field, the cobs high and the dew on
  everything.
- **The man.** Bonesy is a man: short, bow legged, missing teeth, rough hands.
  Nobody speaks.
  - He touches his face with both hands, then his mouth.
  - He looks at his hands a long time.
  - He sits down in the earth between the rows and pushes his fingers into it.
  - He closes his eyes in the sun.
- **What the brothers do.** Jigsaw looks away first; it is too private.
  Crumble watches.
- **The joke, when it comes,** is small and bodily. *Suggested, Aydin will
  write his own:* "Teeth were better than I remember." ... "Not by much."
- **The house** at the edge of the field has smoke coming out of the thatch,
  and someone in the doorway grinding maize. Bonesy will not look at her.
  He gets up and walks the other way, fast. He does not say who she is, and
  the brothers do not ask. This is a hint, not a reveal.
- **The jars,** *option.* By the path stand big clay jars sealed with wax, the
  same as the jar from the cave. Bonesy carried jars like these down to the
  priests' cellars, so the farmer and the "barrels" are one man. Nobody says
  it.

### Four roads (6.3)

- **The crossroads.** Crumble names the colours aloud, red, white, black and
  yellow, and he is pleased to know something on this road.
- **The black road speaks.** It sounds like a friend, and it says the way is
  short.
- **The fifth road.** Bonesy stands looking at the roots of a ceiba. "There's
  another." Nobody else sees it until he walks onto it. It goes down.
- **The weight.** Crumble: "He never told me about a fifth." That is the first
  crack, and he doesn't know yet how big it is.

### The student (6.4)

- **The face is never seen.**
  - **Always turned away.** He sits with his back to the doorway. When he
    turns, he turns away from them, or smoke is between. The road will not
    show what the dead you carry did not want to remember. *Invented.*
  - **What the brothers see.** His hands are clean, never worked, a scholar's
    hands. Jigsaw marked the spy at Petit-Goâve by hands like these, and he
    dislikes this man for it before he has heard him speak. That is the wound
    talking, not a clue to Spain.
  - **His Maya is good,** better than Crumble's. Crumble hears it and says
    nothing.
- **The work** must be concrete, not slogans.
  - Balam is teaching him to quiet a fire with his breath and the right words,
    a small working. The fire in the hearth goes low and blue.
  - The student does it, and then does what Balam did not teach. He takes the
    fire in the house next door with it as well, and a woman shouts.
  - Balam is proud for a moment. Then he is not.
- **The argument** comes out of that, not out of nowhere.
  - **Balam:** "A keeper does not own what he keeps."
  - **Student:** "Then why should he keep it?"
  - The student is talking about the knowledge; Balam is talking about the
    fire, and the woman's supper.
  - "If a promise can be made, it can be broken" fits only if Balam has made
    him promise something on the spot. Use it there or not at all.
- **Jigsaw.** The student's line lands on him. He thinks it is a fair
  question. He does not say so; it shows in what he notices.

### The burning (6.5)

- **The ordinary day.**
  - Women at the grinding stones, a man mending a net, dogs.
  - A child runs to the edge of the trees because she knows the voice
    calling a greeting.
  - The student comes in out of the forest alone, on foot. Someone asks where
    he has been. An old woman scolds him for being thin.
  - **Jigsaw, who has spent a chapter learning a village like this, sees the
    soldiers' boats before anyone in the vision does.** He sees the shape of
    an ambush, his own trade, and he shouts, and nobody can hear him. This is
    the brief's helplessness made specific to his skill.
- **The stillness.**
  - The hand goes under the cloak, and the air stops. The smoke from the
    cook fires stands straight up and does not lean.
  - Bonesy feels it first. In the vision he has a body, and his body goes
    heavy, like the bottom going out of a boat. He sits down hard.
  - None of them hears a sound. It is not a bell yet.
- **The failing.**
  - A woman breathes at a canoe to push it off, and nothing moves.
  - A man with a turtle's back sets himself in a doorway, and the soldiers'
    pikes go into him as if into anyone.
  - A boy whose change the reader never learns tries something, and stands
    surprised.
- **The fighting.** The villagers fight with what they have: digging sticks,
  stone, fishing spears. Some of them kill soldiers. They are not lambs.
- **Balam.**
  - His working, *comparison E*, rises and fails in the stillness. Crumble
    sees it and goes white.
  - Balam goes on with a blade, which is the style Crumble knows. Crumble
    watches his own forms done better than he has ever done them.
- **The confrontation** is in the open ground by the burning houses.
  - "You brought them here." The student does not deny it.
  - He asks for something. *Comparison F.*
  - Balam draws. The hand goes under the cloak again. For the first time Balam
    is afraid. "I taught you everything I knew." "That was your first mistake."
  - The vision tears like wet paper, before the student turns his face.

### The drawer (6.6)

- **The shop.** Jigsaw is alone, in the shop, at the counter. The brown jar is
  on the shelf. He is not seventeen; he is himself, now, too big for the room.
- **Not a rerun of 5.1.** There is no dialogue about price.
- **The drawer.** The apothecary opens a drawer under the counter, and in it
  lies the leaf. Jigsaw reaches. The apothecary closes the drawer on his
  fingers, not hard. "It isn't yours."
- **The hands.** Jigsaw looks at his hands and they are red to the wrist.
  Somewhere in the shop the student's voice says, "Then why should he keep
  it?"
- **Bonesy.** The bell on the shop door rings. *Option: avoid any bell here,
  since the black bell is the later payoff. Use the door's hinge.* A farmer's
  rough hand comes down on Jigsaw's shoulder, as it did on the rock before the
  pool. Bonesy does not explain anything. *Suggested, Aydin to write:*
  "Shop's shut." ... "Come on, Jig."
- **The lilies.** Outside the shop door is the lake, and the lilies are
  beginning to open.

### What the ceremony gives, and what it does not

This follows the brief's lists, kept.

**The quest takes from it:**
- Amara's practical clue, comparison C.
- The fifth road, unexplained.
- The stillness, unnamed.

**Nobody takes from it:**
- the student's face;
- the bell;
- what the codex does;
- why the student did it;
- what became of Balam after.

## 4. Weaknesses to fix before drafting

1. **Two big visions in one book.** Chapter 6 (the ceremony) and chapter 9
   (the hold, now renumbered) are both drugged visions with the brothers side
   by side. **Fix:** make chapter 9 shorter and different in kind.
   - It is forced, not chosen.
   - It shows their own lives, not Balam's.
   - Bonesy is absent, in the chest.
   - Cárdenas writes it down.

   Or cut the hold vision to Jigsaw's life only, since Crumble's past with
   Balam has now been seen. **Decide.**
2. **The ceremony could answer too much.** The rules above limit it. The road
   shows only what the dead you carry saw, and it cannot be questioned.
3. **Amara could become an exposition machine.** She has a real source (her
   father's book) and real limits: she reads the glyphs but not the sense.
   She needs Jigsaw's sea sense to finish the clue.
4. **The student could be guessed too early.** The clean hands, the good Maya
   and the burning by soldiers all point at a Spaniard. Some readers will
   guess. That is acceptable as dramatic irony if the crew cannot guess. If no one in the book has yet seen Cárdenas's face (check chapters 2 and 3),
   nobody has a face to match. The hidden face and the plain cloak keep the reader from certainty.
5. **The patrol's timing.**
   - The root man's sensing gives the warning in the night.
   - The launch must still be far enough away for the scouting and the plan,
     and close enough to arrive in the afternoon rain.
   - So the root man feels it at the river's mouth before dawn, half a day of
     rowing below the bend.
   - The scouting party reaches the long reach by mid morning, and the rain
     comes mid afternoon.
   - The drafted 5.3 had the villagers arrive "in the middle of the morning"
     and then wait for hours, which is loose. Retime it: the scouts go at
     dawn, the bowmen and spearmen follow at noon.
6. **Why the patrol comes at all.** The village is not hidden by magic. The
   patrol is following the river mouths (the old chain: Domingo, then the
   fishing canoe). That chain is in the unread packet. It still works.
7. **Jigsaw's arc pressure.** The ceremony shows him the student's ownership
   argument and his own red hands. Then at the bend he kills men with their
   hands up. If the burning showed soldiers killing people who trusted them,
   Jigsaw at the bend is doing the soldiers' work. That lands without a word,
   and it means the ceremony must not overstate it.
8. **Crumble's arc.** The ceremony gives him the "why didn't he teach me"
   wound. That deepens his bible arc, from worthiness to Balam's distrust, but
   the answer has to be different from what he fears. Balam held back to keep
   him safe, not because he was unworthy. Save that answer for his chapter 9
   conversation with Balam.
9. **Bonesy's turn as guide must not make him wise.** He guides by instinct, he
   does not understand it, and he says so. His lines stay short.

## 5. Comparisons where the best choice is not obvious

### C. The practical clue Amara gets

|---|---|
