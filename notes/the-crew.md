# The crew

Jotted by Aydin, 2026-10-03. Ideas, not yet in the bible.

## In his words

Jigsaw and Crumble have each other's backs. Alone, each of them is fine.
Together they give each other energy. Like dapping up one of your long term
boys when you see him at the club: the dap itself gives you something. That is
the connection.

Bonesy is the guy where you look over and think, what is this man doing. He
supports, but Jigsaw and Crumble know the mission and Bonesy is tagging along,
fucking around. All three love each other, and it shows in motion.

## What that means on the page

**Jigsaw and Crumble are better together than apart.** Not only loyal. Lifted.
Each is a capable man on his own. Side by side they get faster, looser, surer,
a little cocky. The other man being there is fuel.

**They carry the mission.** The codex is theirs. Crumble's through Balam,
Jigsaw's through the door. They plan it, argue it, and carry the weight of it.

**Bonesy is along for the company.** He does not much care about the codex. He
cares about them. He wanders off, courts statues, cheers a butchering like a
cockfight, and pitches in when it counts. The reader should regularly look up
and think, what is he doing now.

**All three love each other and none of them says it.** It shows in motion.
Rule 6 already demands that. This note is about which motion.

## How the dap reads in 1690

The dap is modern. The feeling is not. Ways it can show without leaving the
century:

- A look across a deck before going in, and both go.
- Finishing the other's work without being asked. A line made fast, a man taken
  off his back.
- A hand at the back of the neck or on a shoulder going past, quick and gone.
- A dry line one of them only says because the other is there to hear it.
- Jigsaw's narration taking plain pleasure in watching Crumble be good at
  something.

The last one carries the most weight. The book is in Jigsaw's head, so the lift
has to show in how he narrates Crumble.

## What the draft already has

The backs are covered. The fights show it:

- 3.4: *We closed the boat from both ends toward the middle.*
- 3.5: *Crumble from one side, me from the other.* And: *Another would swing at
  where I had been and find Crumble instead.* That line is the whole idea in
  one sentence.
- 3.5: *Crumble went first.* Then, *I came after.*
- 2.4: the wrist over the rail, held longer than it needed.

## What the draft is short on

The lift. Nearly every quiet beat between them is grief. Watching him go up the
hill, the not asking, the silences, Domingo. The bible frames their bond almost
entirely as a shared wound, witnessed not discussed. That is true and it stays.
But this note asks for the other half, two men who are glad of each other, and
the draft has very little of that.

Places it could land without restructuring anything:

- 3.3 into 3.4, where the trap is decided *without a word*. That wordless
  agreement could carry a beat of plain confidence.
- 3.5, the moment before they go down the shroud.
- 4.1, eleven days in a small boat. Room for them to be easy with each other
  instead of only heavy.

## How it sits with the bible

Nothing here contradicts it. It adds to it.

- The mirror section is about the wound they share. This adds the lift. Both
  are true. The wound is why they hold on. The lift is why holding on feels
  good.
- The bible calls Bonesy the secret heart and says he already knows what the
  other two are chasing. That fits him tagging along. He is not there for the
  codex. He is there because they are the first people who would buy him a
  hat. He does not need the mission. He already has what the mission is for.
