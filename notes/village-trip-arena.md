# The village trip: three pitches and a judged road

2026-10-28. Aydin asked for the patrol to come later, after more village life,
and for the village to help the crew with the quest through a psychedelic trip.
The trip should slowly reveal the codex and how it protects the village, and
give a hint of Balam's failed student without naming him. Cárdenas names him
later. Three pitches came from three lenses. Nothing here is decided.
Everything marked *invented* is open to veto.

---

## The three pitches in brief

### A. The Road Under the Lilies (mythic lens)
Models: the Popol Vuh road to Xibalba, the Water of Life in *Dune*, *Pan's
Labyrinth*.

- **The drink.** Ix Kab, the hive keeper (*suggested name*), proposes the trip.
  She is the woman of "old business" and a survivor of Balam's burned
  settlement. The drink is balché with a water lily steeped in it, plus
  tobacco and lime. They bleed an earlobe onto bark paper with a stingray
  spine.
- **The rite.** The brothers lie in two dugouts among the lilies. Bonesy holds
  both lines. The rule is that nobody lets go.
- **The road.** It goes down to the dead, who show only what is theirs. The
  brothers take the red road east, the rule from the leaf in 4.6. They see:
  - **The breaking.** The old priests cut the book at its folds. One has bark
    hands with a band of plain skin where the book lay.
  - **The count.** A keeper names the day at dawn, and Crumble says the same
    name with him. Crumble's dawn habit is the living day count, unbroken for
    centuries.
  - **The rite.** The priests teach the spoken rite to a child as a story. It
    is the root grandfather's story from 5.2.
  - **The blind.** The codex's "before" magic hides the river past the dead
    tree. It opens behind a leaf like a wake, so the crew's leaf let the patrol
    in.
  - **The shrine.** It sings eleven breaths and is silent three. You go in on
    the silence.
  - **The cellar.** Centuries later a short, bow legged man sits on a jar of
    balché and laughs high.
- **The failed student hint.** "He gave you the count. The first one, he gave
  both."

### B. The Dark Lessons (wound lens)
Models: *A Wizard of Earthsea*, the cave on Dagobah, the Animus in *Black Flag*.

- **The rite.** It happens in Balam's empty house. Amara proposes it because
  the sign is in her father's hand.
- **Crumble.** His body relives the blindfold lessons Balam gave him at night,
  and Balam's corrections come out of Crumble's own mouth. The lesson about
  the singing stone breaks off: "Not this. Not again." Once Balam calls him by
  another student's name.
- **Jigsaw.** The drink shows him the village made plain and he wants it.
  Blind Crumble hits him with the stick. Jigsaw pins him with a knee, then
  stops when Bonesy says his name.
- **The failed student hint.** A practice stick in the rafters is worn to a
  bigger hand and wrapped in a gentleman's Spanish wool.

### C. The Long Way (community lens)
Models: *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade*, *Arrival*, the moon letters in
*The Hobbit*, *The Name of the Rose*.

- **The brew.** The whole island brews balché over three days, and every
  changed person gives real work:
  - honey from the hives;
  - bark cut by the jaguar shadow man;
  - coral from the coral man, burned for lime. Jigsaw does the cutting.
  - the claw woman on the drum;
  - the drinking vessel, raised from the lake floor by the fisherman and
    Bonesy.
- **The rite.** The root grandfather tells "the long way," his story's full
  form, and makes Jigsaw drink to hear it. When the drum stops, everything
  stops. Jigsaw strikes once more after it stops, and the circle laughs. Ah
  Pech stays sober to keep the fire.
- **The reveal.** The fable is a bowl that makes the river run back, broken by
  old men and carried away by canoe.
- **The moon letter rule.** Each leaf can be read whole only in its own place
  and time.
- **The patrol** arrives the next morning on a sick, emptied island.

## Where all three agree

- **The drink.** Balché, from the village's own stingless bee hives and
  balché bark. Brewing it takes three days, and those days are the extra
  village life Aydin asked for.
- **Bonesy.** The drink runs out through his ribs ("Wasted on me"). He stays
  sober and hears every word said aloud, which keeps chapter 7 true.
- **Amara** does not drink. She stays on this side and keeps watch over them.
- **Ah Pech** is there and stays sober, listening to drugged men. That is what
  Cárdenas does in 7.3, and nobody notices yet.
- **Balam does not appear.** His one appearance stays in chapter 7.
- **What the codex does** is never said. "Before" stays an image until 7.2.
- **The patrol** comes after the trip, and Jigsaw's brutality at the bend
  costs more because the village has just let him in.
- **Balam's settlement.** All three reconcile it the same way, *invented*.
  Cárdenas once stopped soldiers burning it to win Balam's trust. After Balam
  cut him off, he came back and burned it himself. Balam brought his small
  daughter to the lake.
- **Length.** The chapter grows to about 25,000 words. Trim about 800 from the
  middle of 5.2, or split it into two chapters.

## The verdict

**A wins on what Aydin asked for.** It goes back to when the codex was made,
and its reveals are working clues, not lore:
- **The living count.** Crumble's dawn habit turns out to be one of the
  codex's five parts. That is the best single idea in the arena.
- **The rite in the children's story.** This sets up 9.10, where Amara can
  warn Cárdenas his rite is incomplete.
- **The blind.** It answers "how they are protected by it" directly. It also
  turns the patrol into a consequence: the crew's own leaf opened the way.
  Crumble would not lead Cárdenas to her door, and the codex did it for him.
- **The shrine's breaths.** Jigsaw learns that rule through his body, the way
  he found the reef gate in 5.1.

**C wins on how the village takes part.** Aydin wants the village to help.
In C, every changed person brings real work to the rite, and nobody leads it.
Jigsaw cutting the coral man is the deepest trust in the chapter, and it makes
the bend worse. The drum rule ("when it stops, everything stops") plants the
bend without a word.

**B has the best single wound beat, and the riskiest.** "Not this. Not again"
is strong. But Balam's voice coming through Crumble's mouth edges against
"Balam appears once." Jigsaw's vision of the village made plain gives away
what the codex does five chapters early. The Spanish wool on the stick points
at Cárdenas too loudly. Keep one beat: in the trip Jigsaw reaches for Crumble
and stops for him, so the bend shows he cannot stop for strangers. Use it
only if C's drum rule does not do the job alone.

**The judged road takes C's spine and A's road.**

## Tangents worth keeping in view

- **The way comes home (A, C).** On the road they pass a jaguar walking. At
  dawn the man asleep two months wakes and names the day, or names the
  Spaniards past the dead tree before the boys do. This is Aydin's call on a
  character's fate.
- **The other end (A, B).** Classic Maya vases show balché taken by enema.
  Bonesy has never been happier. It fits the book's register, though the
  practice is uncertain by 1670.
- **The ballgame (A).** The dead make Jigsaw lose a game on purpose in front
  of Crumble to earn the shrine. It prices his wound exactly.
- **The villagers glimpse "before" (C).** The root grandfather walks in the
  drink and comes back not wanting it: "Couldn't feel the children." This is
  the strongest scene in the arena, but it spends the 7.2 reveal early.
- **The stick fits the wrong hand (B).** Jigsaw picks up the first student's
  stick and it fits him better than Crumble. That plants the mirror Cárdenas
  holds up in 8.2.
