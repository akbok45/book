# The back half: three pitches and a judged road

2026-10-11. Three competing pitches for book one from scene 4.3 to the end,
each from a different shelf of comparable books, judged against the settled
facts in `manuscript/STORYBOARD.md`. Nothing here is decided. Aydin chooses.
Everything marked *invented* came from a pitch and is open to veto.

---

## The three pitches in brief

### A. The Hold (captivity lens)
Models: *The Empire Strikes Back*, *The Count of Monte Cristo*, the last act of
*Gideon the Ninth*.

- The cave is a false victory. The bearing is split: Crumble reads a star
  glyph, Jigsaw reads the current. Bonesy: "Took two of you."
- A Spanish picket herds them. They take a merchant for water and joy.
- The second mark is empty and too quiet. Cárdenas has sat there for days and
  cannot open it. They are taken.
- In the hold Domingo plays Faria. Ruined, he gives up the one thing he never
  sold: where the second mark's water comes out.
- **Ending: the brothers escape through the water. Bonesy stays behind, shut
  in the gold house that held the finger.** He chooses it: "Go." Crumble puts
  the tiller over to follow Cárdenas's wake. Book two is *go get Bonesy*.
- About 54,000 words.

### B. Company (bond and tone lens)
Models: *Gideon the Ninth*, *The Lies of Locke Lamora*, the Aubrey and Maturin
novels.

- The cave holds a niche with three hand hollows. It opens only when Bonesy,
  off courting a carving, puts his bone hand in the third.
- A quiet night over the Spanish charts. Jigsaw takes plain pleasure in
  watching Crumble work.
- The merchant raid is a con. Bonesy plays plague dead in the bilge, and
  Jigsaw hails in the school Castilian of the class that beat him. Then the
  joke turns: the hold carries Maya sacred things consigned to Cárdenas, and
  the news that Domingo broke.
- Bonesy puts a stone he loves over the side, for the living.
- In the hold Bonesy slips his irons the first night and puts them back on.
  Staying is his choice.
- **Ending: all three escape with the second piece, which opens for their
  three hands mid fight while Cárdenas watches. He keeps the first leaf. His
  stern lantern never moves.** Jigsaw knows they were let go. Crumble lays a
  course on an unrolled chart for the first time in the book: "I went."
- About 56,000 words.

### C. The Race (adventure lens)
Models: *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *Treasure Island*, *On Stranger Tides*,
*Pirate Latitudes*.

- The cave's box is sealed with lime and carries one glyph, *k'al*, binding,
  from the real Maya stone binding rites. The friars wrote it down as *to
  shut*. Bonesy: "Tied, then." ... "Not shut."
- The leaves give a window, a day count only the two can read together.
- The merchant is a Campeche coaster carrying Cárdenas's stores. It holds
  three things. One is the Madrid letter. Another is seized ololiuhqui seed,
  which becomes the dose. The last is a carved Maya piece that locks Bonesy
  rigid, a plant for the capture.
- The mark is Cozumel, Ixchel's old pilgrim island (*invented placement*).
  Fresh graves show his men tried without the count. Cárdenas waits on a camp
  stool at the lip.
- **Ending: all three escape with the first piece. Cárdenas keeps the second,
  and he heard Crumble read its bearing aloud to a dead man in the vision.**
  They run east before a norther with talk of Morgan gathering at
  Île-à-Vache.
- About 67,000 words.

## Where all three agree

When three different shelves reach the same answer, it is probably the
book's answer.

- **The cave is a drowned passage to an air chamber** with a piece of bark
  book in a sealed container. Jigsaw leans toward his door and does not go.
  Crumble pays for hauling him back.
- **The cave plants the bond without saying it.** Nobody explains. Bonesy
  gives the only gloss, in a peasant's word.
- **The merchant raid sits between the cave and the second mark.** It brings
  water, the news of Madrid, and proof that Cárdenas is ahead. It carries the
  lift from `notes/the-crew.md`, the two side by side, fast and cocky.
- **They are taken by an officer's Maya artifact.** It drops Crumble and
  Bonesy. Jigsaw stands outside its reach, could run, and lays his blades
  down. The abandoned one does not abandon.
- **Cárdenas tells Crumble about Domingo with Domingo in the room.** The four
  planted lines in 2.2 and 3.1 turn over.
- **The vision:** Balam asks Crumble why he is still carrying the work. The
  father says his saying and nothing more. Bonesy takes the dose and nothing
  happens, and he complains he was left out.
- **Crumble's reframe is acted, not said.** He leaves Domingo a second time,
  on purpose, looking at him. Bonesy models it with *Poor trade.* ... *Still.*
- **Cárdenas never chases.** He sits, listens, writes, and lets the lantern
  stand.

---

## The verdict

**Strongest ending: A.** Bonesy in the gold house pays a line already on the
page, 1.2: *I've been in a gold house. I didn't care for it.* It turns his
wound, the first thing over the side, into a choice he makes for them. It
gives book two the plainest engine there is. And it opens the trilogy's
question at its hardest: men who have only ever been left now have to go back
for someone. The risk is that the brothers seem to abandon him. The fix is
that he sends them, and the reader sees it.

**Best cave plant: C's *k'al*, with B's three-body mechanics.** A word with a
second meaning is subtler than three hand hollows, which risk explaining book
three. But the cave should still need all three bodies, so Bonesy is part of
the key and not a spectator.

**Best merchant raid: B's con, with C's cargo.** Bonesy playing plague dead
and Jigsaw wearing his enemy's accent is the most fun and the most character.
C's crate for Don Mateo carries the most plot.

**Best dose: C's ololiuhqui over datura.** It is real, Mexican, and banned by
the Inquisition in New Spain from 1620. A Christian ideologue keeping seized
heathen seed in his cabinet is Cárdenas in one object.

**Best quiet scene: B's night over the charts.** The draft is short on the
two being glad of each other, and this gives it a whole scene.

**Best image: B's stern lantern that never moves.**

## The judged road

About 42,000 new words, for a book near 58,000. The craft guide sets a first
novel floor nearer 70,000. Pitch B notes that the thin early scenes, 2.1 at
351 words, are where growth belongs. Do not pad the back half.

### Chapter 4, The Half Moon, continued
- **4.3 The Pull.** Bonesy goes down the pull on a line, since he does not
  breathe, and comes back with *There's stone.* The passage takes two breaths
  for a living man. Jigsaw dives the line with Bonesy holding the slack on the
  bottom and Crumble paying out from the rock. Past the chamber the water goes
  on down, still pulling. Jigsaw leans toward it. The line comes taut.
  Crumble's palms are raw, and nobody mentions it.
- **4.4 The Chamber.** A lime-sealed box with folded bark leaves. The *k'al*
  glyph, and Bonesy's *Tied, then.* ... *Not shut.* Crumble reads what it
  says and Jigsaw reads where it points. Neither half alone is a bearing. The
  tide traps them for hours they cannot spare. Bonesy sits one breath with a
  carved face on the wall and leaves her.
- **4.5 Spent.** The pull stops. The tarpon go out through a boil past the
  break, and Jigsaw finally sees where the water goes. The cask he lived off
  for five years is broached. A topsail shows on the rim.

### Chapter 5, The Run
- **5.1 Soundings.** A quiet night over the Spanish charts. Jigsaw watches
  Crumble be good at the work. The water sums come up short for the leg.
- **5.2 The Fever Ship.** The con, then boarding from both ends toward the
  middle. Bonesy turns up in the master's periwig under his hat.
- **5.3 Consigned.** The midpoint turn. A crate for Don Mateo holds three
  things: ololiuhqui seed, a stone face Bonesy once sat with, and a Maya
  piece that locks Bonesy rigid until Jigsaw drags it off him. The Madrid
  letter. The master's log shows a Spanish warship on the same course a week
  ahead, every sail set through a blow. Jigsaw reads Spanish and keeps the
  log line to himself.
- **5.4 Over the Side.** A norther they have to run from. Bonesy puts the
  stone face over the side himself, better for her than a gold house. *Poor
  trade.* ... *Still.* Crumble watches with the chart in his hands.

### Chapter 6, The Second Mark
- **6.1 Would He Break.** Bonesy pries at Crumble about Domingo. Crumble takes
  a turn off a line that did not need taking.
- **6.2 The Lip.** His ship already at anchor. The site opened and scored by
  chisels, the box still whole, fresh graves above the beach. They open it
  at the window, because only they can, and climb out with the second piece.
  Cárdenas is on a camp stool.
- **6.3 Taken.** The officer's artifact, and what it costs him, which Jigsaw
  clocks the way he clocked the spy's hands. Crumble and Bonesy go down.
  Jigsaw lays his blades on the sand.

### Chapter 7, The Hold
- **7.1 The Cabinet.** Cárdenas's room and shelf: the obsidian mirror, the
  quipu, the empty gold reliquary from Veracruz. His courtesy.
- **7.2 Domingo.** Brought in alive, hands ruined. Cárdenas thanks him for the
  meeting, in front of Crumble. Domingo says only that he did not sell the
  bearing. The not being dead line from the continuity flags can land here,
  in a scene with air.
- **7.3 The Dose.** Ololiuhqui. Balam's question. The father's saying. Bonesy
  left out, sitting between them with a hand round each wrist. Cárdenas
  writes it all down.
- **7.4 What He Heard.** Cárdenas has their wounds now, and he knows the cave
  took more than one man. He questions Bonesy about the priests' cellar.
  Bonesy remembers only the barrel he sat on.
- **7.5 The Names.** Domingo gives up where the second mark's water comes out.
  Crumble answers him in Maya, and Jigsaw catches three names: Ixmal, his
  own, Bonesy's. Bonesy: *Go.* He puts the hat in Crumble's hands. *Mind
  that.*

### Chapter 8, The Mouth
- **8.1 The Mouth.** Out through the water, the one place blood and coin count
  for nothing. Crumble leaves Domingo and Bonesy behind on purpose, with his
  eyes open. Cárdenas's bet, made from what he heard in 7.3, was that the man
  who left once will never leave anyone again. That bet fails.
- **8.2 The Lantern.** Two men in a small boat with the hat in Crumble's lap.
  The stern lantern behind them never moves. Crumble unrolls the chart for the
  first time in the book and lays a course on Cárdenas's wake. On the
  horizon, talk of Morgan gathering at Île-à-Vache.
