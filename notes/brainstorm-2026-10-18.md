# Brainstorm: ways to make book one stronger

2026-10-18. Ideas only. Nothing here is decided and nothing has gone into the
story doc. Each idea says what it is, which story it borrows from, why it helps,
and where it would land. Aydin picks.

Ranked by how much each one does for the book against how little it costs.

---

## The ten strongest

### 1. Cárdenas uses the vision against them, and it fails

**What.** In 7.3 and 7.4 Cárdenas writes down everything they say aloud while
drugged. In 8.5, when he questions Crumble and Amara separately, he uses it. He
tells Crumble that Balam called him his weakest student. He brings up Anne. It
does not work, because the vision already gave Crumble Balam's real answer.

**Borrowed from.** O'Brien in *1984*, who knows exactly what Winston fears. Here
the same move fails.

**Why.** It makes the vision the direct cause of Cárdenas failing to break
them. Right now the vision and the prison sit side by side. This chains them.

**Where.** 8.5. One paragraph.

### 2. Cárdenas hears that Balam called him his greatest mistake

**What.** Crumble talks in his sleep during the vision. Cárdenas, writing it
down, hears Crumble ask Balam why he taught Cárdenas, and hears enough of the
answer to know Balam regretted him. Cárdenas calls Balam the most important
teacher of his life. Now he knows Balam ranked him last.

**Borrowed from.** Obi Wan, Anakin and Luke. Two students of one teacher, one
who frees himself from needing the teacher's approval and one who never does.

**Why.** It makes Cárdenas a mirror of Crumble as well as Jigsaw. Both are
sons of Balam trying to earn him. Crumble learns he does not have to become
Balam. Cárdenas never stops trying to prove Balam wrong. It also explains why
in Florida he insists on speaking the rite himself: Balam taught him, and he
will not let anyone say Balam taught him badly.

**Where.** 7.4, then his anger in 8.4 and his stubbornness in 9.10.

### 3. A real clock: the morning star

**What.** Venus vanishes near the sun for about a week, then rises before dawn
on one particular morning. If that morning is missed, the next one is about
584 days later. The Maya tracked this exactly. The Venus table in the Dresden
Codex, one of the four Maya books that survive, counts it in cycles of 584
days. So the Venus page has a deadline.

**Borrowed from.** Durin's Day in *The Hobbit*, already the model for the clue
chain. The door only shows on one evening.

**Why.** It gives the prison a ticking clock. Cárdenas bargains in 8.6 partly
because the morning is coming and breaking them is too slow. It gives chapter 9
a hard race. It costs nothing new, because the Venus page already exists.

**Where.** 8.5, 8.6, and the opening of chapter 9.

### 4. Put the prison in Cárdenas's own fort near Veracruz

**What.** Chapter 8 is set at Campeche. In 1670 Campeche was close to
undefended. It was sacked in 1663 with only a militia to defend it, and its
walls were not begun until 1686. The fort they robbed in chapter 1 is near
Veracruz, and San Juan de Ulúa, the island fortress in Veracruz harbor, was a
real fort and later a notorious prison. Move the fort there.

**Borrowed from.** *There and Back Again*. The story comes round to where it
started.

**Why.** It is more accurate. It brings them back to the cellar where Bonesy
stole the finger, which Jigsaw can recognize. It makes Cárdenas's power
concrete: this is his house.

**Where.** Chapter 8, plus `reference/world-1670.md`. Check the drafted chapter 1
description of the fort before deciding.

### 5. Pay off the gold

**What.** In 1.1 the young priests say gold was buried with every piece. The
outline never mentions gold again. Readers will notice. Three options:

- **Small and real.** The jar in 4.6 holds a few gold pieces. Enough to buy new
  gear at Tortuga in 9.1, not enough to make anyone rich. Jigsaw, who wants
  wealth as proof he mattered, gets a handful.
- **A priests' lie.** The old priests told the young ones about gold to keep the
  keepers loyal. Bonesy thinks this is the funniest thing he has ever heard.
- **Gold lost in the end.** The Florida temple really holds gold, and breaking
  the codex sends it into the deep with the leaves. They laugh about it, the
  way the men laugh at the end of *The Treasure of the Sierra Madre* when the
  gold dust blows away.

**Why.** It closes an open promise from page one, and options one and three
feed Jigsaw's arc about what is worth something.

**Where.** 4.6, 9.1, or 9.12.

### 6. Rodrigo's wife at Cárdenas's table

**What.** During the privileges in 8.2, Cárdenas seats Rodrigo's wife at
dinner. She is the girl Jigsaw loved. She has a child. Later she tells Jigsaw
plainly why she left: not only Rodrigo's rank but his own anger.

**Borrowed from.** *The Count of Monte Cristo*. Mercédès marries Fernand, the
rival who rose, and meets Edmond again years later.

**Why.** The bible says the two men's lost women rhyme: each found her settled
with another man and a child. Crumble gets that scene in the vision. This gives
Jigsaw the same beat in the present, used as a weapon by Cárdenas, and it
supports the outline's point that Rodrigo is partly right.

**Where.** 8.2. One dinner.

### 7. The elder turns because of what the codex does

**What.** The elder made his deal to keep his people alive. He does not know
the codex would kill many of them. In Florida, during their fight in 9.8,
Crumble tells him. That breaks the reason for his deal, and he turns. He is the
one who warns Cárdenas in 9.10 that the rite is incomplete, and Cárdenas
ignores him. Afterward Amara, as her people's leader, decides what happens to
him.

**Borrowed from.** Lando Calrissian in *The Empire Strikes Back*, who sells out
his friends to protect his city, then turns when the deal keeps getting worse.
Also Long John Silver.

**Why.** It answers the open question of whether he turns, with a cause rather
than a change of heart. It gives Amara a hard leadership choice at the end.

**Where.** 9.8, 9.10, 9.14.

### 8. Bonesy's face

**What.** In 9.11, as the restoration starts, Bonesy's bones begin to grow
back the man he was: short, bow legged, a gap in his teeth. For a moment
everyone sees the face from the vision. Then the man underneath starts to die,
because he died centuries ago. Bonesy chooses to stay bones and scatters the
leaves. He keeps a permanent mark from it: two fingers, or a hand, that never
come back.

**Borrowed from.** The moonlight reveal in *Pirates of the Caribbean*, turned
inside out. Note that in that film lifting the curse also kills the cursed.
Worth keeping our version clearly different: our people do not want to be
restored.

**Why.** It pays off the vision image, makes restoration visible in one body
the reader loves, and gives the ending a cost that lasts.

**Where.** 9.11 and 9.12.

### 9. The one object without a label

**What.** In Cárdenas's cabin every object has a label except one, a small
token of his sister's: a ribbon, a rosary, a child's shoe. Jigsaw notices and
says nothing. In 9.11 the codex turns the collection back into plain stone,
wood and bone. Her token was never magic, so it survives. He is left holding
the only thing that was ever his.

**Borrowed from.** Rosebud in *Citizen Kane*. Miss Havisham's stopped clocks.

**Why.** It shows his grief in an object, which the prose laws prefer to any
speech. It makes his loss in Florida personal as well as practical.

**Where.** 7.1 and 9.13.

### 10. The Calusa

**What.** The temple is in Calusa country, and the outline has no Calusa in
it. Historically they controlled the coastal waterways of south Florida and
resisted Spain for more than two hundred years. In chapter 9 they can be a third
force. Cárdenas forces Calusa pilots to guide his boats, and they lead him
wrong. The crew has to deal with them too, and neither side gets a free
helper.

**Borrowed from.** The real history, and the Ewoks done properly: people with
their own reasons who are not there to serve the heroes.

**Why.** It is accurate. It gives a believable reason Cárdenas's boats struggle,
which comes straight from his flaw: he forces people instead of trusting them.

**Where.** 9.3 to 9.5. Needs careful research.

## More ideas, by area

### The setting

- **The sinkhole is real.** Warm Mineral Springs and Little Salt Spring, near
  Charlotte Harbor in old Calusa country, are deep sinkholes with ledges partway
  down. Human remains around 10,000 years old have been found on those ledges.
  The temple's ledge could hold the bones of the first keepers, and Bonesy is at
  home among them. One catch: these are freshwater springs, not tidal. Either the
  tide flooding the sinkhole is part of the magic, or the timed window becomes
  something else, such as the first light of the morning star falling down the
  shaft onto the stone.
- **Bishop Landa.** In 1562 Diego de Landa burned Maya books at Maní. Only four
  Maya books survive today. Cárdenas can see himself as Landa's smarter heir.
  A line idea for him: Landa burned them, and I read them first.
- **The Madrid Codex was really in two pieces.** It surfaced in Spain in the
  1800s as two separate manuscripts, found apart and recognized as one book. A
  real broken codex, worth knowing for book three.

### Cárdenas

- **Glimpsed early.** In 3.1 Crumble says the Spaniard in the clean coat was
  looking at the trees, right at him. In chapter 7 Cárdenas can reveal it was
  him: he was there at the meeting. That puts him on the page in chapter 3 with
  no change to the drafted prose. Borrowed from the shark in *Jaws*, felt long
  before it is seen.
- **Orders found on the dead captain.** When they strip the ship in 4.1 they
  could find Cárdenas's written orders: take the two men, and take the dead
  one alive. The first sign he wants Bonesy, in his own handwriting.

### Bonesy

- **Bonesy present while absent.** In chapter 8 Bonesy is in a chest on
  Cozumel for some 15,000 words. Cárdenas can bring him up at the dinners:
  reports from his guards that the skeleton asked for a candle, a woman and a
  deck of cards. Jigsaw laughs at Cárdenas's table for the first time.
  Cárdenas sees what loyalty looks like and cannot use it. This keeps the
  comedy alive through the darkest stretch.
- **The hat.** Crumble carries Bonesy's hat through chapter 8. Cárdenas asks
  about it. At the rescue Crumble puts it back on Bonesy's skull without a
  word.
- **A stolen idol.** In 7.1 Bonesy flirts with a small stone woman on
  Cárdenas's shelf and pockets her. She is the one labeled object that walks
  out of the collection.

### Jigsaw

- **The ring and the hidden brotherhood.** The bible gives Jigsaw his father's
  ring and a secret brotherhood of corsair exiles. None of it is in the book one
  outline yet. Light ways in: the father wears the ring in step 1 of the
  vision; at Tortuga in 9.1 a smith recognizes it and gives him the corsair
  blade. That opens book two without explaining anything.
- **A sea fight in the back half.** It is a pirate book with one ship fight, in
  chapter 3. At the Florida estuary mouth, a night raid in the style of the
  cutting out raids in *Master and Commander*: Jigsaw times the falling tide and
  cuts one of Cárdenas's ships loose to drift aground. It shows his seamanship
  one last time and adds pace before the mangroves.
- **He stops counting.** Early on, Jigsaw's narration prices everything:
  shares, coin, what a man is worth. By the end of the book he no longer does.
  A voice habit that carries his arc without a word of explanation.

### Crumble

- **The day count after the end.** He keeps the count as penance for Balam. In
  the aftermath he can keep it because he wants to, or stop. Either choice shows
  the arc.

### Amara

- **Plant her want early.** In the village she asks Jigsaw about open water and
  watches the stars from a roof at night. Then her wish in 8.8 to sail north
  until the stars change has roots. Borrowed from Éowyn, who fears a cage.
- **Give her a mistake.** She saw the elder was afraid and decided to carry his
  fear herself instead of asking about it. Her flaw costs her people something.

### Structure

- **Sag risk in chapters 7 and 8.** Together they are about 35,000 words of
  captivity. *The Count of Monte Cristo* makes prison work because the hero
  learns something there; our vision does that. The morning star clock (idea 3)
  and Bonesy at the dinners help. Worth checking each scene changes something.
- **A cold open.** Low priority. *Gideon the Ninth* opens with an escape and
  *The Lies of Locke Lamora* with a con. A two page opening of the Veracruz
  cellar job, with Jigsaw calling it off as the storm hits and Bonesy lagging
  behind out of sight, would open on action and keep the 1.2 reveal intact. It
  means new prose before 1.1.
