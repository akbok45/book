# Style rules, with worked examples

The short version lives in `CLAUDE.md`. This is the same set with the reasoning and examples from the draft itself, for when a call is close.

---

## 1. No filler

A word that could come out without loss must come out. The draft is already good at this. Compare a sentence carrying nothing spare:

> The wind drew in a long breath. Held it. Let it out a touch softer than the breath before.

Three sentences, no adjective doing decoration, and the weather has become a body. Nothing to cut.

Filler is not only adjectives. It is the sentence that restates what the last sentence already did, the beat that explains the beat before it, and the clause that hedges. *Almost*, *somewhat*, *a bit*, *rather*, *quite*, *very*, *seemed to*, *began to*, *started to*.

## 2. The word "thing"

Out of the narration entirely. It is allowed inside a character's mouth where the man would reach for it, and Bonesy would. Jigsaw's narration reaching for *thing* is the narration failing to name what it saw.

Two places the draft earns an exception and should keep them, because the vagueness is the point:
- *"the thing I have never been able to name"*, where naming it would kill the hunger.
- *"the thing forty years of Cardenas has been spent trying to scrub off the earth"*, where the vagueness is the contempt.

Everywhere else, name the object.

## 3. Analogies from their world only

The test: could a man on that deck in 1690 have made this comparison out of what he has seen. If it needs a machine, a science, a city, or an idea from after 1690, it is out.

Working examples from the draft, all legal:

> a hermit crab will go bare and pinched its whole life before it climbs into a shell that is not its own
> the way a ladyfish will throw itself clear of the water like a tarpon and fool you for half a breath
> He fights like a snare taking shut.
> patient as a hook in deep water
> hung still in the water with everything in it pointed at you

Note what these have in common. They are not decoration. Each one carries information the plain sentence could not: what Bonesy is, how a lie works at a distance, how a man kills.

Illegal, even though it sounds period: anything drawing on clockwork, machinery, chemistry, medicine past bleeding and amputation, or a metaphor of the mind as a container.

## 4. Two commas, maximum

The rule kills runoff. It is a cap on commas, not on length, and there are two exceptions.

**Lists** are exempt. *Powder, flints, wadding, a ball mould and bar lead.*

**A short appositive dropped in for weight** does not count against the pair:

> I took his throat with the short blade, sawed it, felt the gristle of the windpipe part under the edge

and the pattern he named it by:

> scabbard, clean, the hand dropping with the hilt still in its grip

When a sentence wants a third comma and is not one of the two exceptions, it is two sentences. The draft does this constantly and it is why the prose hits:

> He nodded. He closed his eyes. He took another small piece off the leaf.

## 5. 1690 is locked

Every object, word, food, weapon, coin, and idea must have existed. `reference/world-1690.md` carries the detail: what they ate, what they were paid in, what the articles said, what a sloop could do that a frigate could not.

The things that break the spell fastest are small. Boot laces. A fair. A word like *focus* or *contact*. The ban list in `CLAUDE.md` is the search and destroy pass.

**Gun** means cannon in this century. A hand weapon is a pistol, a musket, or a piece.

**Bonesy is exempt on register.** He can sound a century early and it reads as him rather than as a slip, because he is a walking dead man with no stake in how a room expects him to talk. *Hello, fellas* is his. The exemption covers diction and idiom only. He cannot name a made object, a trade, or an idea that has not been invented, because that breaks the world rather than the manners.

## 6. Feeling in behavior

The rule the whole book runs on. A wound is never announced. It shows in the hands, the jaw, where the eyes go, what gets reached for.

> He closed his eyes again. He did not look surprised. He looked like a man who had been holding his breath without knowing it and had only just been able to let it out.

> Crumble took the chart from under his coat and held it without unrolling it, which is the most of an answer Bonesy was going to get

> and he did not argue it, and that was the thing I marked, because Bonesy argues everything

Three men, three wounds touched, no man says a word about how he feels.

The corollary: **do not explain the beat after it lands.** If the behavior did the work, the sentence that follows must move, not summarize.

## The scene machine

Most scenes with the three run on the same engine, and knowing which instrument is playing keeps the voices from blurring.

**Bonesy drives the noise.** He pries. He says what the two repressed men will not. No shame, no stake, which is the tool that opened their wounds in the first place.

**Crumble bleeds when pried.** He shuts the door, says it is fine, reaches for a task, the tiller, a line, the chart. Write the reach for the task, then write the task failing to save him.

**Jigsaw threads.** He speaks a little, then the narration turns the moment outward to the world or inward to himself. When it touches his own wound it should read personal in the moment, not as a reread surprise.

## The one line test

Before any of the three speaks or moves: is the feeling shown in behavior, is every comparison from their world, and is the voice on the right rung of the ladder. If yes, it holds.
