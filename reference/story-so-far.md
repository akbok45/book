## The Story So Far (Book One)

**The world.** The Caribbean, 1690. The buccaneer era is fading but still alive. Tortuga still runs as a French-held pirate outpost. The Maya are still resisting Spanish conquest inland. The supernatural exists but is rare, seedy, and mostly ignored, woven into commerce and vice rather than wonder. The book is narrated first-person by Jigsaw, in a plain sailor's voice.

**The opening.** Three mercenaries, Jigsaw, Crumble, and Old Bonesy, shelter in an abandoned Maya temple on a jungle island, waiting out a storm. They've just failed a smuggling job: they were hired to steal a saint's finger-bone reliquary from a Spanish fort near Veracruz, but Jigsaw read a storm coming and judged they didn't have time to search the vaults, so they walked away empty-handed. Their stolen Bermuda sloop is tucked in a protected cove nearby.

In the temple, Bonesy entertains himself by carrying on a one-sided romance with a stone carving he's named Beatriz (insisting she's a rain goddess; Crumble corrects that it's a corn god, a man). Through Bonesy's needling and the pressure of close quarters, the codex surfaces: Crumble's late teacher Balam told him of a Maya codex, broken apart on purpose during the conquest and scattered in sequence so no one could take it whole. Jigsaw realizes the first piece sits in a place he visited nine years ago, a half-moon island with a tidal pool full of impossibly large tarpon, water that moves wrong, a cave hidden behind something the Maya couldn't name. Three pieces of knowledge converge: Crumble's history, Jigsaw's waters, Bonesy's centuries of wandering.

**The Margaret reveal.** As they prepare to leave, Bonesy reveals he actually did secure the finger-bone during the Veracruz job, slipping away unnoticed while the others argued the weather. He let them mourn the failure for two days because it amused him and because he wanted them clear of the fort before producing it. He'd freed it from its gilt reliquary ("no one's so dead they need a house with no door"). The job isn't a failure after all, they can get paid. They leave the island, Bonesy giving the stone Beatriz a quiet, audience-less goodbye.

**Tortuga.** Nine days' sail. On the way, they confirm the Veracruz fort belonged to Don Mateo de Cárdenas, a Spanish colonial officer they've crossed twice before. At Tortuga they deliver the finger to the hire's agent (Vaz) and get paid. They outfit the ship and, almost casually, buy Bonesy his first-ever clothes, a hat, a belt, a blade, the quiet emotional core of the section: no one had ever thought of him as someone you dress, someone who belongs among the living.

They split up. Crumble goes to meet Domingo, an old Maya contact who knew Balam as a boy. Jigsaw and Bonesy go to the one tavern, where Jigsaw clocks a too-clean Spanish face, a spy who's been tailing them, who overhears enough (a Maya book, a Maya contact) that Cárdenas, a Thrawn-like collector of his enemies' histories, will assemble the whole picture from fragments. The Tortuga code forbids Jigsaw from acting.

**The escape.** Cárdenas's money turns the French garrison. Crumble, meeting Domingo out in the forest, gets only part of the confirmation, the codex is real, broken in sequence, the cave is the first mark, before soldiers come. Crumble escapes with a smoke screen; Domingo is taken so Crumble can get away. Jigsaw runs for the ship, not knowing if his brother lives. They slip out clean because Tortuga won't allow killing in the harbour, but they've left their water and provisions on the quay. Now they're being hunted, under-provisioned, and quietly aware that their one advantage, outrunning weather in a small fast ship, means nothing against Spanish ships that sail straight through storms. They set course for a hidden cay to refuel, then on to the half-moon island and the first piece of the codex, going in blind to the one thing Domingo never got to tell them.

## Character Deep Dive

### Jigsaw — narrator, the one who sees

The perceptive one. His domain is the physical world: water, weather, currents, the small wrong thing in a room. He keeps the trio alive by reading what others miss. His narration is plain, observational, withholding, a sailor's mouth, no modern phrasing, analogies drawn always from ocean and nature.

**The wound:** he was rejected by someone he loved, the abandoned one. (Specifics to emerge organically in scenes.) This makes him watchful, self-contained, the man who notices everything and says little.

**The deeper hunger:** beneath the adventure and the gold, Jigsaw is searching for meaning. He's chased the spiritual unknown his whole life, the "door" at the bottom of the sea he stood at when he was twenty and didn't go through. He frames it as adventure but it's a search for something he can't name. He's the one who wants the codex most, which is exactly why his counsel of caution carries weight.

**With Crumble:** about seven years of brotherhood, from meeting as very young men at Tortuga, with the years they spent apart folded in the middle. They communicate in silences and unspoken things they hand each other and never say aloud. Jigsaw watches Crumble go "until I can't."

**Appearance:** 6'2", razor-shaved head, scar on his face, hide-built pirate gear (hide sewn into a working coat, not worn like a hunter), twin lighter Spanish arming swords instead of a cutlass. A legend follows him from a galleon he cleared single-handed.

### Crumble — the Maya-trained one, the keeper of history

Roughly 5'11". His domain is the cultural and historical: the Maya language, the old stone, the codex, what things mean. Trained by Balam for three years in his late teens, after he and Jigsaw had already been at sea together. He carries the codex knowledge passively, never hunted it, just held it, until Balam's death (about a year and four months back) gave it nowhere to go.

**The wound:** he is the rejecter. He threw away someone who loved him (a woman or childhood figure, to emerge in scenes). Where Jigsaw was abandoned, Crumble abandoned. This is the mirror at the heart of their brotherhood.

**The deeper hunger:** like Jigsaw, he's searching for meaning, his through fulfilling Balam's unfinished work, honoring the teacher, proving he's not the kind of man who discards people. The codex is his test of loyalty.

**Defiance:** he wears the Maya openly, panels and a deep-red sash over European clothes, a blade Balam had made for him, walking into Spanish rooms looking like the thing Cárdenas spent forty years trying to erase. The bravest small thing Jigsaw knows a man to do daily.

**Voice:** terse, controlled, a man sitting at his own wake. Speaks Maya with a softening only the trusted hear.

### Old Bonesy — the immortal, the comic engine, the secret heart

A walking, cursed skeleton. Was an ordinary Maya peasant before the curse, never important, worked a patch of corn ground, kept out of the priests' back cellars where he first overheard talk of the codex. Doesn't know how long ago. Doesn't care how he was cursed. He loves being what he is.

**The voice:** high-pitched, raspy, drawn-out. Plain, common vocabulary, never elevated, because he was a peasant, not a wit. Sentences are complete because the making of the voice is work. He's smart and wise but common.

**The comic engine:** he makes objects into companions (Beatriz, the stone Ixchel, the saint's finger he treats as loot). He needles, he performs, he holds the floor on things he has no stake in. He cracks the brothers open, his bits are the vehicle for the genuine emotional moments.

**The wound underneath:** centuries among people who treated him as expendable, the first thing thrown overboard when a boat ran short. He's been "put over the side of more boats than Jigsaw has sailed." He gravitated to stone because stone doesn't leave, doesn't decide he's disposable. His attachment to bones and carvings is a lonely immortal making friends out of things that can't abandon him. With Jigsaw and Crumble he has, for the first time, the real thing, people who respect him, who'd buy him a hat. The hat moment is him being offered a place among the living. He's loosening his grip on the substitutes.

**His role in the theme:** he's the immortal who already learned what the brothers are still chasing, that meaning is found in the bond, in company that means something. He found his long ago. He watches them look for theirs.

### Don Mateo de Cárdenas — the antagonist

Old (50s+, older than J and C, still a dangerous fighter), forty years a Spanish colonial officer. Served in Peru young, then the Maya regions. Not driven by personal vendetta, he's a colonialist ideologue who believes the world should be Spanish and Christian and takes out whatever isn't, with calculated cruelty, torture, breaking the people around his targets.

**The Thrawn dimension:** he collects the histories, books, and sacred artifacts of the peoples he destroys, studies them, understands them better than they understand themselves, because you cannot truly finish a people until you do. This makes him uniquely able to assemble the codex's shape from fragments. He keeps occult artifacts of his own, though curses are anomalous even in this world.

**The threat:** he doesn't appear in chases, he plans, watches, runs spies. He was already ahead of the trio at Tortuga. And he commands ships that sail through storms the trio must flee, nullifying their one advantage.

### The dead and the offscreen

**Balam** — Crumble's teacher, a Maya resistance fighter and master who taught him fighting, stealth, history, and language. Died naturally about a year and four months ago, mid-hunt for the codex. Lives in memory; possibly one ghost appearance later if it earns its place.

**Domingo** — Maya contact at Tortuga, knew Balam as a boy, goes by a Spanish name in port. Taken by Cárdenas's soldiers so Crumble could escape. His fate is now an open thread.

## The Thematic Core

The question the trilogy asks: *can two broken men find something worth living for in each other, and is that enough?* Jigsaw seeks meaning through the spiritual unknown; Crumble through Balam's legacy; both are really seeking the same thing, and it turns out to be the bond between the three of them and Bonesy. That meaning is designed to literally unlock the final codex gate at the trilogy's end, the emotional arc and the mystical mechanic are the same thing.
